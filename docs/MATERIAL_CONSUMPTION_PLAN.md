# Material Consumption — Tying Inventory to Work Orders

Status: **PR 0 merged** (`#154`, inventory hardening) · **PR 1 merged** (`#155`, core engine, shipped dark) · **PR 2 on `feat/material-tie-ux`** (nest tie UX) · **PR 2.5 on `feat/incremental-material-consumption`** (consumption moves to operation completion) · **PR 3 on `feat/material-return-verb`** (the reasoned RETURN verb) · **PR 4 on `feat/backflush-breadth`** (the BOM/routing backflush leg hardened *with the flag still off* — **re-scoped**, see [Delivery](#delivery)) · **PR 4.4 on `feat/backflush-lot-policy`** (one lot-selection policy for both engines + reconcile-to-target on the backflush leg, via a new `work_order_backflush` reference shape — the flag is **still off**, and it adds **no** re-entry trigger) · **PR 4.5 on `feat/backflush-exposure`** (the flag becomes settable behind a readiness refusal gate, plus two pure reads and a completion-time refusal on blocking diagnostics — **default-off, no production part opted in, and sign-off NOT obtained**, see [Exposing the flag](#exposing-the-flag-pr-45)) · PR 5 planned.

## Why this exists

Material was tracked in inventory and consumed on the floor, but nothing connected the two: a laser nest could cut fifty sheets and stock would not move. Owners asked for material to deplete as it is used — the headline case being *when a nest is marked complete, one sheet comes out of sheet inventory*.

> **Resolved in PR 2.5 — and the record of what this said before is kept deliberately.** Through PRs 1 and 2 the headline case above was the ASK, not what shipped, and **this document asserted otherwise for two PRs**: depletion fired only when the **work order** completed, never when a nest did. A laser child work order carries one operation per nest, so marking nest 1 of 3 complete moved no stock at all. That was verified **false** against every call site during PR 2 — after UI copy written from this document came out wrong — and was corrected in place rather than quietly rewritten. **PR 2.5 makes the headline case true:** completing an operation consumes *that operation's* ties, so finishing nest 1 of 3 now deducts nest 1's sheet. What is still **not** true is the finer-grained promise the "three of five sheets across three days" line further down makes: production **reporting** is deliberately not a consumption trigger, so reporting 3 of 6 runs on an unfinished nest still deducts nothing. See [Capability vs. wiring](#capability-vs-wiring--when-consumption-actually-fires).

The requirement that shapes every decision below: **tying material to a work order is never mandatory.** A work order with no tie must behave byte-for-byte as it did before this feature existed. That is asserted by test, not just intended.

## What already existed

A consumption engine was already here and largely unused. `app/services/completion_inventory_service.py` runs on every completion path via `apply_completion_inventory_effects()` and does two things: a finished-goods RECEIVE, and a BOM-component backflush ISSUE. The backflush is gated on `Part.backflush_components`, a column with no schema, endpoint, or UI exposure — so in practice it never ran. Much of this effort is lighting that up and extending it, not building new machinery.

> **Correction of emphasis, PR 4.** "In practice it never ran" is stronger than it reads: the column has **no writer anywhere in `app/`**, so the BOM/routing leg has never executed against production data at all. And "lighting that up" is what PR 4 declined to do — reading the leg first turned up eight structural defects, two of them mis-stating demand by whole multiples, so the flag stayed OFF and the leg was hardened underneath it. See [The BOM/routing backflush leg](#the-bomrouting-backflush-leg-pr-4).
>
> **Superseded in one clause by PR 4.5.** "No writer anywhere in `app/`" stopped being true on 2026-07-27: the flag is now settable on `PUT /parts/{id}` / `PUT /materials/{id}` behind a readiness refusal gate ([Exposing the flag](#exposing-the-flag-pr-45)). What is *still* true, and is the sentence to carry forward, is that **the leg has never executed against production data** — exposure creates the possibility, it does not create a run. Every "unproven" caveat in this document survives exposure unchanged until a real part opts in and a real work order completes.

Three facts constrain the design:

- **The idempotency indexes.** `uq_wo_inventory_issue` permits exactly one ISSUE per (work order, part) where `reference_type='work_order'`. Incremental per-run consumption needs many rows per operation, so it uses a **different reference type** (`work_order_operation`) and sits outside those predicates. The existing guards are untouched. **PR 4.4 added a third shape for the same structural reason** — `work_order_backflush` (`reference_id` = the work order), so the component leg can spill across N lots and post a later top-up row, neither of which is expressible under a one-row-per-(company, WO, part) index. The two indexes are again **untouched**: they keep their exact `041`/`076` definitions, and `_component_already_issued` — still keyed on `reference_type='work_order'` — becomes the permanent fence over the legacy rows they cover. Building this surfaced a dev/test parity bug in those indexes: they declared only `postgresql_where`, so under SQLite they degraded into **full** unique indexes covering every `reference_type` — the harness was enforcing a constraint production does not, and would have rejected the consumption path outright. Migration `076` declares `sqlite_where` from the same predicate constant (Postgres emits zero DDL). See `docs/DEVELOPMENT.md` → Database Migrations.
- **Nest material was free text.** `LaserNest.material` / `thickness` are AI-inferred strings with no foreign key to a part, so nothing could resolve "this nest consumes sheet part X".
- **`quantity_allocated` is a dead column.** It is read by MRP and by `quantity_available`, but never written. Reservations are deliberately deferred (see below).

## Design

### The tie

One table, `work_order_material_allocations`, is the single tie entity for every case: a nest's sheet part (operation-scoped), a production work order's BOM components, and ad-hoc material. It carries planned vs consumed quantity, a unit-of-measure snapshot, an optional pinned lot, and a source (`nest` / `bom` / `manual`).

It is **not** a foreign key on `LaserNest`. Nest import wipes and rebuilds nests and operations wholesale, so consumed-state stored there would be destroyed on re-import — the rebuilt nest would consume from zero and the deleted operation ids would orphan every transaction that referenced them.

Status is the tombstone rather than `SoftDeleteMixin`: these are planning rows, and the compliance record is the ledger plus the audit trail.

### Consumption is sum-delta, not evented

On every completion-path entry, for each open operation-scoped allocation:

```
target = COALESCE(qty_per_run, 1.0) × (op.quantity_complete + op.quantity_scrapped)
delta  = target − allocation.qty_consumed
delta > 0  → post ISSUE for −delta, advance qty_consumed to target
delta ≤ 0  → no-op
```

This is idempotent under **replay** — the target is recomputed from operation state, so a re-entry that sees the previous call's committed `qty_consumed` converges without a new unique index. It runs from **two** seams, both computing that same target: `apply_completion_inventory_effects` reconciles **every** open operation-scoped tie on the work order and rides the five work-order-completion call sites (kiosk clock-out, shop-floor and office operation complete, force-complete, reconcile-on-read); `apply_operation_completion_inventory_effects` (PR 2.5) reconciles **one operation's** ties and rides the four `finalize_operation_completion` call sites. The second gets there earlier and narrower; the first is now also the self-heal behind it.

**The formula is still not a statement of *when* stock moves.** The arithmetic would support "three of five sheets across three days" with no extra hooks — an earlier revision of this document said exactly that, as though it were shipped behavior. PR 2.5 moved the trigger one level down, from work-order completion to **operation** completion, but production *reporting* remains deliberately hook-free: keying 3 of 6 runs on an unfinished nest still moves nothing. The next section is the full history of that claim, its correction, and what PR 2.5 did and did not close; it is the single most misread thing in this feature.

**Concurrency is carried by TWO locks, one per race** (updated 2026-07-31 — this paragraph used to say the work-order lock alone carried it, which was true of same-WO races and silent on the other one). The convergence argument is sequential. Two *simultaneous* completions would both compute the same positive delta and both post: these rows sit outside `uq_wo_inventory_issue` by design, and `WorkOrderMaterialAllocation` has no `version` column.

- **Same work order:** invariant 4 — `WorkOrder` and `WorkOrderOperation` map `version_id_col` directly, so every call site that drives a completion takes the optimistic lock and a stale racer gets `StaleDataError` → 409 before reaching the engine. Treat that lock as load-bearing here; the per-allocation savepoint and the "advance `qty_consumed` only when an insert actually landed" rule are damage control, not a substitute.
- **Different work orders sharing a lot:** the WO lock says nothing about two completions of *different* work orders drawing the same `InventoryItem` row, so the read-modify-write of `quantity_on_hand` now holds `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` row locks on the source lots — `consumable_source_items(..., for_update=True)`, the pinned-lot lookups (both engines), and the return verb's source-row fetch all take them. The lock-ordering rule: the WO lock is always taken first (every entry point already holds it), and each locking *query* acquires its inventory rows in ascending-id order (lock by `id`, re-sort to FIFO in Python — the draw itself is unchanged). That is deadlock **reduction**, not proof: ordering holds per query, and one completion issues several locking queries in sequence, so two transactions interleaving different parts — or a completion racing `/inventory/transfer`'s source+destination pair or a cycle count's bulk lock — can still deadlock. The backstop is Postgres deadlock *detection* (one victim errors, never a hang) plus the engine's own design: the savepoint contains the failure and sum-delta re-posts the missing delta on the next completion or reconcile-on-read. Build on detect-and-converge, not on a no-deadlock guarantee. `with_for_update` is a no-op on the SQLite test backend; `backend/tests/test_inventory_row_locking.py` compiles the queries for the postgresql dialect to pin the `FOR UPDATE`.

Consumption rows post with **`reference_type='work_order_operation'`** and `reference_id` = the **operation** id, deliberately outside the `uq_wo_inventory_*` predicates. Each row also carries `inventory_transactions.allocation_id` — the durable genealogy key that survives an untie, since allocation rows are cancelled, never deleted.

**Scrap consumes.** A scrapped run physically used its sheet. It posts as ISSUE rather than SCRAP, because lot genealogy filters on ISSUE — using SCRAP would make exactly the material most likely to be audited disappear from the traceability record. The good/scrap split is recorded in the transaction `notes`.

**Consumption never auto-reverses.** When a supervisor walks back an over-count, delta goes negative and consumption no-ops; the sheet is already cut. Automatic reversal would let a floor-side quantity edit silently move inventory — and because the consume path also runs from a GET (reconcile-on-read, inside a bare `except: pass`), it would do so with no reason recorded and attributed to whoever happened to be reading the page. Reversal is instead an explicit, reasoned, audited RETURN verb, shipped in PR 3 — see [Returning consumed material](#returning-consumed-material-pr-3). The negative delta the engine computes and refuses to execute is *exactly* what that verb performs, with an actor and a reason attached.

**Shortage never blocks production.** Insufficient stock drives the source lot negative, writes an `ALLOCATION_SHORTAGE` audit row, and emits a warning event (`material_allocation_shortage` → catalog key `material.allocation_shortage`; Purchasing, warning, in-app + email) — the same posture as the existing backflush. Refusing would train operators to untie material.

**A pinned lot is never spilled off, on either tie shape.** Pinning is a lot-directed instruction, so an insufficient pinned lot is driven negative rather than quietly consuming a different (uncertified, wrong-heat) lot. Unpinned ties walk FIFO stock — `received_date ASC NULLS LAST, id ASC` — and spill across lots when the head lot cannot cover the delta. A **work-order-scoped** tie carries its pin into the backflush's tie leg, so the pin means the same thing there as it does per-run.

> **Resolved in PR 4.4 — one lot-selection policy, and the paragraph this replaces was the plan's first residual.** Through PR 4 the unpinned work-order-scoped leg used a *different* selection from the per-run engine: **lowest-`id`**, with **no `status` predicate at all**, a **single** lot for the whole demand. Both legs write lot genealogy, so on the same material they could name **different heats for one physical draw** (AS9100D 8.5.2). Both engines now share `consumable_source_items` — `received_date ASC NULLS LAST, id ASC` over lots that pass one `CONSUMABLE_ITEM_CLAUSES` predicate — and share `plan_stock_draw`, the pure spill arithmetic. The unpinned backflush therefore spills across lots exactly as the per-run engine does, and its shortfall is computed against **the lots it actually walked**.

**A pin bypasses FIFO's *ordering*, not its hold check.** The shared FIFO read (`consumable_source_items`) excludes lots that are inactive or whose `status` is not `available`; the pinned branch reads one named lot and would otherwise consume `on_hold` / `quarantine` / `rejected` material straight into an as-built record (AS9100D 8.7). The control is split in two, because the two moments have different options: **pinning** a held or inactive lot is refused at tie time with **422** (a human is present to answer), while a lot held *after* it was pinned still consumes — refusing from a reconcile-on-read GET would be unattributable and the material is already in the part — and writes a tamper-evident **`HELD_MATERIAL_CONSUMED`** audit row naming the lot, its status, the tie and the quantity, for the segregation review that has to follow. Every leg that can consume a held lot runs `is_consumable_item` and writes that row.

> **`HELD_MATERIAL_CONSUMED` now means one thing only: a PINNED lot that was held after it was pinned.** Through PR 4 it had a second meaning — the unpinned work-order-scoped leg, having no `status` predicate, could *pick* an already-held lot automatically and recorded the fact with `extra_data.pin_directed = false` rather than skipping it. **PR 4.4 removed that call site**: the unpinned draw now skips held and inactive lots, on both engines, which is the AS9100D 8.7-correct behaviour. What replaced it is **disclosure on the shortage row** — when a shortage is recorded on an **unpinned** draw, `held_stock_summary` adds `held_quantity_skipped` + `held_lot_numbers` to the `BACKFLUSH_SHORTAGE` / `ALLOCATION_SHORTAGE` `extra_data` *and* to the description (*"short 40; 60 on hand in segregated status (not drawn), lots …"*), so a part whose stock is entirely quarantined never reports a bare shortage against material physically sitting on the rack. **A PINNED draw discloses the pin instead** (`pinned_lot`, *"draw restricted to pinned lot X, other stock not eligible"*): there the pin is what excluded the other lots, so naming their hold status would state a false cause — it would point an MRB reviewer at quarantined stock whose release changes nothing, and omit the available stock the pin also excluded. `shortage_draw_disclosure` picks between the two; they are mutually exclusive by construction. `pin_directed` survives as a field and is now always `true`.
>
> **Neither clause reaches the as-built record.** Both live in `audit_log` and the event payload only — `_reconstruct_consumed_components` reads `inventory_transactions` — so a genealogy line never names a lot that was skipped, and a part whose stock is wholly held now traces to a **lot-less placeholder** where it previously traced to the held lot. Owner-acceptance item, recorded at [Open questions](#open-questions-carried-into-pr-45) item 6.

> **A legacy NULL-`status` lot IS consumable, and PR 4.4 is where that became explicit.** `inventory_items.status` has a Python-side `default="available"` with no `server_default` and no backfill, so a bare `status = 'available'` predicate would have hidden legacy stock — driving the engine to mint a lot-less placeholder, record a false shortage, and produce a row PR 3 refuses to credit back. The shared clause is `COALESCE(status, 'available') = 'available'`, which says in SQL what `is_consumable_item` already said in Python, compiles identically on Postgres and SQLite, and is a strict *widening* (it never makes a held lot consumable). **This is the one genuinely live behaviour change in PR 4.4** — it reaches the operation-scoped tie engine, which is shipped and running.

> **Disclosure — lot-hold state is not live in-system.** Nothing anywhere in `app/` ever writes a held `InventoryItem.status`: no endpoint or schema exposes the column, it is only ever set to `"available"` at row creation, and there is no verb that deactivates a lot. Both halves of the control above — the 422 pin refusal and the `HELD_MATERIAL_CONSUMED` row — can therefore only fire on data set **outside** the application (a direct DB write, an import, or a future hold verb). They are built and tested ahead of the feature that will produce that state. Do not read them as evidence that quarantine/hold is an operating control today; the hold verb itself is unbuilt.

**Read-safety.** Each allocation's work runs inside its own `begin_nested()` savepoint and the entry point never propagates — the *whole* body, operation lookup included, is under that guard — so a failure degrades that one allocation instead of poisoning the session a reconcile-on-read GET is about to commit. A rolled-back allocation is **not** silent: it writes an **`ALLOCATION_CONSUMPTION_FAILED`** audit row (`success=false`, with the exception text), because material that should have depleted and did not is a worse control gap than the shortage case, which already writes a chain row. `AuditService.log` opens its own savepoint and swallows its own failures, so it is safe to call on the outer transaction after the rollback.

### Capability vs. wiring — when consumption actually fires

**Resolved by PR 2.5. This section is kept, not deleted:** that this document asserted a false headline for two PRs, and how that was caught, is worth more to the next reader than a clean page. The heading is also left unchanged because `docs/API.md`, `docs/KIOSK.md` and two `docs/CMMC_LEVEL_2_COMPLIANCE.md` changelog rows point at it by name, and those rows are historical.

#### The state through PR 2

**The engine was capable of incremental depletion and nothing called it that way.** Those are two different claims, and PRs 0–1 of this document conflated them; PR 2 stated the distinction plainly rather than closing it.

Every call site of `apply_completion_inventory_effects` is gated on the **work order** finishing — that was true then and is still true now:

| Call site | Gate |
|---|---|
| `shop_floor.py` — kiosk clock-out | `if work_order_completed:` |
| `shop_floor.py` — shop-floor operation complete | `if work_order_completed:` |
| `work_orders.py` — office operation complete | `if work_order_completed and work_order:` |
| `work_orders.py` — force-complete | inside the privileged override that itself drives the WO to `COMPLETE` |
| `shop_floor.py` / `work_orders.py` — reconcile-on-read | keyed on `completed_wo_ids`, built from `resource_type == "work_order"` transitions only |

There was no sixth call site of that seam — and, until PR 2.5, no second seam. Because a laser child work order carries **one operation per nest**, finishing nest 1 of 3 deducted **nothing**; all three ties flushed at once when the last operation closed the work order. What sum-delta bought was that the flush is *correct* whenever it lands — replay-safe, reconcile-safe, and right across an operation completed in pieces — not that it lands early.

**PR 2 deliberately shipped truthful copy rather than a new consumption call site.** Every string the tie UX rendered said "deducts N when WO-#### finishes"; none said "deducting now". `frontend/src/utils/materialTie.ts` is the single home for both the client-side sum-delta arithmetic and that wording, so the dispatch chip, the kiosk notice and the Materials panel cannot drift apart or drift from the engine. Wiring incremental consumption inside PR 2 would have changed *when stock moves* for every work order in the shop, inside a PR whose scope was making existing behavior visible.

#### What PR 2.5 changed

**Tied material now depletes when an OPERATION completes** — that operation's ties, and only that operation's. A second, narrower seam does it: `apply_operation_completion_inventory_effects` (`completion_inventory_service.py`) → `consume_tied_materials_for_operation` (`material_consumption_service.py`), wired at the four `finalize_operation_completion` call sites (kiosk clock-out, shop-floor operation complete, office operation complete, force-complete). Finishing nest 1 of 3 now deducts nest 1's sheet, which is the ask this document mis-stated as shipped for two PRs.

The five work-order-completion call sites are untouched: work-order completion still runs the **whole-work-order** reconcile, which is now the self-heal behind the new trigger. A tie already consumed at operation completion computes `delta = 0` there and posts nothing; a tie the operation-scoped seam missed (see the residual gaps below) still flushes at the end, exactly as before.

**Scope is ONE operation, deliberately.** Running the whole-work-order reconcile at operation completion would be the obvious simplification and it is wrong: it would consume against operations still `IN_PROGRESS`, and an in-progress operation is still **reducible**. `production_reduction_service` refused a walk-back with **409** only when `operation.status == COMPLETE` or the work order is terminal — so a supervisor could legitimately reduce an in-progress operation's count *after* material had been posted against it, stranding over-consumed material with no way back: consumption never auto-reverses, and there was no RETURN verb until PR 3. A **completed** operation was reduce-immune at the instant the ISSUE posted — the same 409 that would have let the reduction through a moment earlier now refused it. That immunity was the entire reason the trigger is scoped to the one operation that just completed rather than to the work order it belongs to.

> **PR 3 removed half of that immunity on purpose, and the scope decision survives on a changed rationale — say so rather than leaving the paragraph above reading as still-current.** The **office** verb now accepts a COMPLETE operation (`allow_completed_operation=True`); the operator's shop-floor verb does not, and neither accepts a terminal work order. So a completed operation is no longer reduce-immune to a supervisor. What makes that safe is the thing that was missing when the paragraph above was written: the stranded material now has a way back, and lowering a completed operation's count is precisely what *opens* the bounded `correct_over_consumption` allowance the RETURN verb is measured against. The one-operation scope still holds, for the part of the argument that did not change — an `IN_PROGRESS` operation is reducible by **either** verb, including the operator's own self-service one, so consuming against it would still hand an operator a way to silently strand material with no supervisor in the loop.



**Still NOT per-run.** Production reporting is deliberately not a consumption trigger. Reporting 3 of 6 runs on a nest whose operation is still open deducts nothing; the sheets move when the operation closes. So the **"three of five sheets across three days"** line this document once used to describe shipped behavior is **still false**, and saying that plainly is the point of this paragraph: the earlier overstatements were caught only because somebody re-read the document against the call sites, and quietly leaving the last one standing now that the others are resolved is precisely how the same failure recurs.

**Two call-site details are load-bearing, not incidental.** (1) The consumption call sits **before** each handler's work-order-completion block, so `apply_completion_cost_rollup` sees the new ledger rows on the *same* request rather than a request later — a nest's material lands in `WorkOrder.actual_cost` when the nest closes, not when somebody next loads the page. (2) Each call site issues a `db.flush()` first. The engine runs SELECTs while the `version_id_col`-mapped `WorkOrder` / `WorkOrderOperation` rows are dirty, and an autoflush `StaleDataError` raised *inside* the engine's per-allocation savepoint would be caught there and degraded into an `ALLOCATION_CONSUMPTION_FAILED` audit row — swallowing the optimistic-lock conflict that is supposed to surface as the handler's documented **409** (invariant 4). Flushing first makes the conflict raise where the handler can translate it.

**FG receipt and BOM backflush stay work-order-completion-scoped.** Neither moved. A finished-goods receipt against a half-finished routing would put unbuilt product in stock. **The reason given here for the backflush was superseded by PR 4.4 and is corrected rather than quietly restated:** it used to read *"the BOM backflush is keyed on `uq_wo_inventory_issue` — one ISSUE per (work order, part) — so it has nowhere to put a second, per-operation row"*. That premise is now false — the component leg posts `work_order_backflush`, outside that index, and can write as many rows as it needs. The correct reason is the one that did not change: **its demand basis is the whole job.** `_routing_backflush_demand` reduces whole-job quantities with `max()`, deliberately discarding operation identity, and the BOM explosion has no operation at all — so there is no per-operation quantity to reconcile a per-operation post against. Only the operation-scoped tie leg has one.

**Neither leg runs at all on a laser nest-dispatch WO** (added 2026-07-31, from the inventory audit). `receive_finished_goods_for_work_order` and the BOM half of `backflush_components_for_work_order` both early-out on `is_laser_dispatch_work_order` (`work_order_type='laser_cutting'` — parented children *and* part-less standalones). A nest-dispatch WO is a dispatch pool, not a unit of product: its `quantity_complete` counts pooled nest **runs**, and a parented child carries the **parent assembly's** `part_id` (`_ensure_laser_child_work_order`), so the FG receipt was minting phantom finished goods of the parent part (5 nests × 8 runs = 40 phantom units) that the parent's own completion later books for real, and an armed parent part would have exploded the parent's BOM against nest-run counts. Only the BOM leg is gated on the backflush side — material **ties** (operation- and work-order-scoped alike) are the nest flow's actual consumption mechanism and post unchanged. `WorkOrderCreate` now also refuses `work_order_type='laser_cutting'` (422), so an API client cannot hand-mint a WO that these skips would silently apply to; nest-dispatch WOs are born only through the internal import paths.

### Residual gaps of the operation-completion trigger

Four things PR 2.5 does **not** fix. None justifies holding the change; each will read as a bug to somebody on the floor, so they are written down here rather than discovered in production.

- **Force-complete consumes nothing.** `complete_work_order` force-closes every still-open operation and stamps its status, timestamps and actor — but it never writes `operation.quantity_complete`, and `finalize_operation_completion` does not write it either (it rolls the operation up and syncs the *work-order* quantity). So a force-completed operation reaches the engine at `quantity_complete == 0`, therefore `target == 0`, therefore its tie posts **no ledger row at all** even though the engine ran on it. The call site is wired anyway, for symmetry with the other three and so the seam is not the thing that has to change later. This is **pre-existing** — the work-order-completion trigger had the identical hole — and **no hook placement fixes it**: nothing can consume against a quantity nobody recorded. The fix is force-complete writing the operation quantities it is implicitly asserting, which is a separate product decision about production-record honesty (a manager's "close it out" is not the same record as an operator's count, and AS9100D 8.5.1 cares about the difference); inventing a quantity here would deplete material for runs nobody reported, which is the worse error. Expect it to surface the first time a manager force-closes a tied laser work order and no sheets move. An operation that *did* carry produced quantity from an earlier partial report still flushes normally.
- **Reconcile-on-read can flip an operation COMPLETE without the work order completing — and that operation's tie does not consume.** `_copy_slot_completion_evidence` will complete a regenerated operation row from a sibling's evidence and record a `work_order_operation` transition, while `_apply_reconcile_inventory_effects` keys only on `resource_type == "work_order"` transitions. So such an operation's tie waits for work-order completion. That is **deliberate**, on the rule this feature has held since PR 1: consuming from a GET has no actor, no intent and no reason to record, and the never-auto-reverse posture makes an unattributable deduction expensive. It is recorded as a known residual, not as an oversight — the whole-work-order reconcile still catches it at the end.
- **`consumed > target` is now an ordinary steady state, not a rarity.** The office operation-complete path writes `quantity_scrapped` **absolutely** (it can move it down), and both reduce-production verbs lower `quantity_complete`; either drives `target` below the tie's `qty_consumed`. The engine handles it correctly — `delta <= 0` is a no-op, never an auto-reversal (invariant 6b) — but when consumption happened once, at the end, this was an edge case. Now that material posts at each operation's completion, a tie sitting at `qty_consumed > target` is a normal thing to see in the data, and reading it as corruption would be a mistake. **Closed by PR 3:** the gap between `qty_consumed` and `target` is exactly the `correct_over_consumption` allowance, so an over-consumed tie now has a bounded, reasoned, audited way back to `qty_consumed == target`. This entry stays because the *state* is still ordinary — a return is a decision somebody makes, not something the system does for them.
- **Nest re-import bites once any nest completes, not once the whole job does — and PR 3 does NOT unlock it.** ⚠️ **Correction: an earlier revision of this document promised that it would.** Re-import is refused **409** on any tie the **ledger** references (`ledger_backed_allocation_ids`, re-keyed off the `qty_consumed` cache in PR 3 — see [Lifecycle interactions](#lifecycle-interactions-settled-during-implementation)). Previously that refusal only became reachable after the whole work order completed — by which point re-importing a nest package was already unusual. Now the first completed nest on a three-nest job locks the package for the other two, and **the remedy remains opening a new work order**. Why a return cannot be the remedy, and why building it that way would have been worse than not building it: a full `return_and_untie` drives `qty_consumed` to 0, but the original ISSUE row **and** the new RETURN row both still carry `reference_type='work_order_operation'` with `reference_id` = the operation the rebuild is about to delete. `work_order_ledger_filter` resolves operation ids through a **live subquery** over `work_order_operations`, so those rows would not merely lose a label — they would silently drop out of job cost, analytics and lot genealogy while remaining in the ledger, leaving an as-built record that disagrees with the ledger it summarizes (AS9100D 8.5.2). The FK also carries no `ON DELETE`, so on Postgres the delete raises `IntegrityError` and the import endpoint turns it into a misleading **400** — the exact bug class that already shipped through one review because SQLite does not enforce foreign keys. **Returning the material makes the tie's forward-looking demand go away; it does not, and cannot, un-write the movement history that names those operations.** The real remedy is structural and unbuilt: **supersede** operations on re-import instead of deleting them (a new revision of the operation row, with the ledger's references left pointing at the superseded one), which is a routing-revision-shaped change well outside PR 3. Recorded here so it is picked up as its own PR rather than re-promised.

### No double-issue (as implemented)

The two tie shapes are handled by different machinery, and the split is load-bearing:

- **Operation-scoped** ties are owned by the per-run engine. Backflush **drops those parts from its demand** (`_drop_allocation_covered_parts`) — nothing at the DB level would stop a WO-level ISSUE from also issuing the same material, because the consumption rows sit outside `uq_wo_inventory_issue`'s predicate. Only operation-scoped ties are dropped, **and only when the operation they name is still on this work order** — the identical check the consume path makes. Without that symmetry a tie pointing at a vanished operation suppressed the backflush *and* never consumed, so the part was silently neither issued nor depleted.

  **That layer is status-keyed, and PR 4 established it is not sufficient on its own.** A tie that consumed and is no longer `OPEN` is invisible to it, and neither `_component_already_issued` nor `uq_wo_inventory_issue` can see tied consumption either — so the part left stock twice, under two different lots, in the as-built record. A second, **ledger-keyed** layer (`_drop_ledger_covered_parts`) now runs alongside it. Both are load-bearing: the status layer suppresses an OPEN tie that has not consumed **yet**, which no ledger read can see. See [The BOM/routing backflush leg](#the-bomrouting-backflush-leg-pr-4).

  > ⚠️ **`_drop_ledger_covered_parts` must keep reading the OPERATION shape ONLY — do not "fix" it to see `work_order_backflush`.** It asks *"has a tie already drawn this part?"* and treats a positive net as yes. Since PR 4.4 the BOM leg reconciles against its **own** history, so if this suppression learned the backflush shape the leg's first post would suppress every later pass **and** emit a spurious `BACKFLUSH_DOUBLE_ISSUE_BLOCKED` row while doing it. It reads `operation_scoped_net_issued_by_part`; the BOM leg reads the separate `backflush_net_issued_by_part`. The two are deliberately different functions over one extracted signed-`CASE` body, and that is why.
- **Work-order-scoped** ties never reach the per-run engine. They are drained by the completion backflush, **as their own leg** (PR 4.4). ⚠️ **This bullet used to say they "merge into the existing one-shot backflush and are summed per part with any BOM demand, because `uq_wo_inventory_issue` permits only one ISSUE row per (work order, part)". That is no longer true and the summing code (`WorkOrderMaterialAllocationDemand`, `_work_order_scoped_allocation_demand`) is deleted.** The two demand sources are now reconciled separately and posted as separately-attributable rows: the tie leg targets `qty_planned` against `net_consumed_quantity_for_allocation` (keyed on `allocation_id`, signed ISSUE − RETURN), the BOM leg targets the resolved demand against `backflush_net_issued_by_part` (`work_order_backflush` rows with `allocation_id IS NULL`). **The total issued for a part carrying both is unchanged — still BOM + tie** — but it is two rows, each walkable to its own origin, and the tie's lot pin now governs the tie's quantity rather than the whole sum. The tie leg runs **first**, deliberately: explicit planner intent outranks derived demand, so a shortage lands on the derived side and the pin gets first claim on stock. Summing was not merely redundant, it was arithmetically wrong under reconciliation — BOM demand is an absolute target while tie demand was a remainder, so summing them and subtracting a net double-counts the tie forever. The tie's `qty_consumed` still advances **only when an ISSUE row actually landed** (`posted_any`), and since PR 4.4 it is set to that tie's **own signed ledger net**, not to `qty_planned`.
- **A work-order-scoped tie consumes ungated by `Part.backflush_components`.** The BOM/routing leg still requires that opt-in flag, but an explicit tie *is* its own opt-in — so a tied part consumes even on a part that never enabled backflush.

### Known limitations

- **A work-order-scoped tie on a part carrying a LEGACY one-shot issue can never consume — so it is refused, not created. The refusal is also UNREACHABLE, and PR 4.4 says so on the record rather than leaving the stated reason wrong.** `_component_already_issued` short-circuits on any existing `reference_type='work_order'` ISSUE for that (WO, part), and `uq_wo_inventory_issue` physically forbids the second row that would fix it. Since PR 4.4 **nothing writes that shape** — the backflush posts `work_order_backflush` — so the guard matches only pre-4.4 rows and is that work order's permanent fence out of the reconciling engine. It is *unreachable* because creating a tie requires a **non-terminal** work order, a `work_order`-shaped component ISSUE requires the backflush, the backflush only runs at COMPLETE, and COMPLETE → non-terminal is blocked. It is kept anyway: one existence query, fails safe, and correct if that reachability argument ever stops holding. Its **wording and docstrings were corrected** (*"already has a one-time issue recorded against work order …"*) — shipping a refusal whose stated reason is false is a records-integrity defect even when nobody can trigger it — and its **behaviour was not changed**. Still tie material **before** the work order completes.
- **A tie on a TERMINAL work order is refused (409)** on one ground only: *a tie that can never consume is a lie*. It would sit `open` at `qty_consumed` 0 forever, indistinguishable in the API from one that simply has not consumed yet, advertising demand that will never be met. The refusal is deliberately **not** justified by claiming every completion path guards on `TERMINAL_WO_STATUSES` — force-complete, for one, refuses a terminal work order through its own explicit checks (`work_orders.py` no-ops COMPLETE/CLOSED and 409s CANCELLED) rather than that constant, so the mechanism claim was wrong even though the conclusion holds. Existing ties on a work order that later goes terminal stay readable and editable.
- **`AllocationStatus.CLOSED` is never written.** Nothing here closes a tie on full consumption or on work-order completion; ties end their life `open` or `cancelled`. The status exists in the model for later PRs.
- **Foreign keys are unenforced in the test suite.** SQLite defaults `PRAGMA foreign_keys` to **OFF** and nothing in `app/db/database.py` or `tests/` turns it on, while production runs on Postgres where they are always enforced. Any "delete a parent row a child still references" bug is therefore structurally invisible to pytest — which is how the nest-re-import FK violation (below) shipped through a first review. One test, `test_operation_delete_after_tie_cancel_survives_foreign_key_enforcement`, enables the pragma for its own body with a positive control; enabling it suite-wide is a separate, larger change.

### Lifecycle interactions (settled during implementation)

- **Nest re-import** resolves the operations it will wipe **before** deleting anything. A tie the **ledger** references on one of them → **409**, nothing destroyed; ties with no ledger row are cancelled with an audit row (`reason: "superseded_by_reimport"`) **and detached** — `work_order_operation_id` is set NULL.

  **The guard reads the ledger, not `qty_consumed` — re-keyed in PR 3, and the difference is not cosmetic.** It now shares `ledger_backed_allocation_ids` with the hard-delete guard, which had used the ledger since PR 1. A full `return_and_untie` drives the cache to 0 while leaving both the ISSUE and the RETURN rows pointing at the operation the rebuild deletes, so a cache-keyed guard would have waved exactly the case PR 3 created straight through. See the fourth [residual gap](#residual-gaps-of-the-operation-completion-trigger) for what that would have cost. The detach is not cosmetic: that FK carries no `ON DELETE` and the model declares no parent backref, so SQLAlchemy will not null it and the very next `db.delete(operation)` raises `IntegrityError` on Postgres, which the import endpoint turns into a misleading **400** ("a nest conflicts with an existing record"). The documented cancel-and-rebuild path was unreachable in production on any laser WO carrying a tie — the feature's headline flow — and unenforced foreign keys hid it from the suite. Nulling is safe for exactly these rows: they are `cancelled`, so neither partial unique index applies, and they carry no consumption (guarded above), so no ledger row references the operation id being dropped. The tie's original scope is preserved on the hash chain (`old_values.work_order_operation_id` and `extra_data.work_order_operation_id`).

  **The wipe reads EVERY status, not just the live ties.** An already-`cancelled` tie still holds the FK — a manual untie and the work-order soft delete both keep `work_order_operation_id` by design — so a status-filtered wipe left the crash reachable through supported verbs alone (tie → untie → re-import), permanently un-rebuildable for that work order. It also **bypassed the consumed-material 409**: the soft delete cancels every open tie *regardless of* `qty_consumed`, so a tie carrying real consumption could reach the wipe as a `cancelled` row the guard never inspected, and its operation was deleted out from under the ISSUE rows carrying that operation's lot genealogy. Reading every status widens the guard correctly and detaches every row. A detach of an already-cancelled tie writes its **own** chain row — a `log_update` recording `work_order_operation_id: <old> → None` with `reason: "superseded_by_reimport"` — deliberately an `UPDATE`, not a second `DELETE`, because the restore discriminator below reads the tie's most recent `DELETE` row. The API echoes the same fact as `detached_from_operation_id` so a detached tie is not read back as one that was always work-order-scoped.

- **A soft-deleted laser child work order is never rebuilt.** Find-or-create (`_ensure_laser_child_work_order`) filters `is_deleted = false`; a parent-addressed import or manual nest-add whose only laser child is soft-deleted gets **409** naming the work order and the restore remedy. Without the filter the import resolved the deleted child and force-set it back to `released` — a deleted work order back on the floor with none of the restore path's controls (no audited `restore`, and `reopen_allocations_cancelled_by_delete` never ran, so it produced with its material demand still cancelled). It is also how a `cancelled`-with-consumption tie reached the operation wipe. A second child is deliberately **not** created alongside the deleted one: that would fork the parent's nest and tie history in two.
- **Work-order soft delete** is never refused for a tie: open ties are auto-cancelled (audited) and posted consumption stands. `work_order_operation_id` is deliberately **left intact** here — a soft delete removes no operation rows, so there is nothing to detach from.
- **Work-order restore un-cancels exactly what the delete cancelled.** Restore is the delete's inverse, so it re-opens those ties (audited, `action="restore"`). Leaving them cancelled meant the restored work order completed and its tied material silently never depleted — with no UI in PR 1, the only signal was a `cancelled` row. It gets worse once `backflush_components` is exposed: an operation-scoped tie that consumed and was then cancelled by a delete/restore round trip stops suppressing the BOM backflush in `_drop_allocation_covered_parts`, so the same part is issued **twice**. **PR 4 closed that second-order failure at its root** — `_drop_ledger_covered_parts` suppresses on the ledger, so a consumed tie in *any* status (cancelled by a delete that was never restored, by a manual untie, or by a hand-edited row) still blocks the double-issue and records the block. The restore behavior below is unchanged and still wanted; it is simply no longer the only thing standing between a delete/restore round trip and a duplicated as-built line. Only ties cancelled *by that delete* come back — a manual untie and a nest-re-import supersede were cancelled for reasons a restore does not undo. The discriminator is the cancel's own audit row: every cancel path stamps `extra_data.reason`, and a tie's most recent `DELETE` row is what its current `cancelled` state means. If that audit row is missing the tie is simply not reopened (the conservative direction).
- **Hard delete** (draft/cancelled only) **409s** when the **ledger** actually references a tie on the work order, since the delete physically removes the operations and ties those rows point at; unconsumed ties go with the work order, audited first. The guard reads `inventory_transactions.allocation_id` rather than the `qty_consumed` cache — the cache is explicitly non-authoritative and the FK has no `ON DELETE`, so keying on it would turn any drift into a 500 instead of the intended 409. **A return does not clear this either**, for the same reason as the re-import: a RETURN row carries the compensated ISSUE's `allocation_id`, so a fully-returned tie is still ledger-backed. The 409's detail says so and names **soft delete** as the remedy (PR 3 reworded it — it used to say "Reverse consumption first", which now names a verb that exists and still would not help).

### API surface

`GET` / `POST` / `PATCH` / `DELETE` on `/api/v1/work-orders/{id}/material-allocations[/{allocation_id}]`, plus PR 3's `GET …/{allocation_id}/consumption` and `POST …/{allocation_id}/return` — a sibling router under the `/work-orders` prefix. Reads are open to any authenticated tenant user; every mutating verb is `require_role([ADMIN, MANAGER, SUPERVISOR])`, deliberately outside the kiosk path fence. Untie is `status = cancelled`, never a physical delete, and is refused **409** while the **ledger** still holds material out against the tie (the 409 names `return_and_untie`, which returns and cancels in one transaction). **PR 4 re-keyed that guard from the `qty_consumed` cache to the signed ledger net** — ISSUE minus RETURN — so a fully-returned tie is untieable; see [The BOM/routing backflush leg](#the-bomrouting-backflush-leg-pr-4) → the one live behavior change. Full request/response shapes and the error table live in `docs/API.md` → Work Orders → "Material ties"; the role rows are in `docs/RBAC_PERMISSIONS.md`. **PR 1 shipped this dark**; PR 2 gave it a UI — the work-order **Materials panel** drives `GET` / `PATCH` / `DELETE`, while the two nest paths below create ties server-side (and, since the work-order **duplicate**, a third server-side creator — see [Where a duplicate's ties come from](#where-a-duplicates-ties-come-from-work-order-duplicate-2026-08)); PR 3 added the return dialog on that same panel. The read-only floor surfaces (dispatch chip, kiosk line) deliberately call **none** of these: they ride the shop-floor reads, which is what keeps the kiosk path fence unwidened.

**Exactly one verb on this router posts inventory: the return.** Every other endpoint manages the planning row alone and consumption still rides the completion call sites only. The return is the deliberate exception, because un-consuming can never be something a completion path does for you (invariant 6b). **PR 4.5 added `GET …/{work_order_id}/backflush-preview` to this same router** — the BOM/routing leg's dry run, placed here because this is where the read-only pre-confirm precedent already lives, and it posts nothing either.

**The BOM/routing half's surface is on the Parts router, not this one** (PR 4.5): the opt-in is a field on `PartUpdate` written through `PUT /parts/{id}` or `PUT /materials/{id}` behind one shared refusal gate, and `GET /parts/{id}/backflush-readiness` is its structured explanation. There is deliberately **no** `POST /parts/{id}/backflush` — the owner chose the ordinary part-edit field over a dedicated reasoned verb, with the residuals that follow recorded in [Exposing the flag](#exposing-the-flag-pr-45).

### Where a nest tie is created (PR 2)

A nest's tie is created **server-side, inside the import transaction** — not by a follow-up `POST` from the wizard. `LaserNestImportRow` and `LaserNestManualCreate` each gained `material_part_id` + `qty_per_run`, and both paths run the one `create_nest_material_allocation` seam in `laser_nest_service.py`, so an imported tie and a hand-created one produce byte-identical rows and identical hash-chain entries.

The alternative — let the client import, then `POST` a tie per nest — was rejected because **the import is atomic and the follow-up would not be.** A failed or abandoned second call leaves nests on the floor that a planner believes are tied and that will never deplete, and there is no compensating verb to clean it up until PR 3. Doing it inside the transaction means a bad part id is a clean **404 with nothing persisted**: every distinct `material_part_id` is pre-resolved tenant-scoped *before* the rebuild wipes the prior nests, so the failure can never land mid-build.

Two constraints on that seam: it must run **after** the operation wipe and `cancel_allocations_for_operations` (a superseded tie is cancelled, never deleted, so `uq_wo_material_alloc_open_op` must only ever see one OPEN row per key), and it ships **unpinned** — FIFO picks the lot at consume time, since planner/operator lot-picking is deferred. There is deliberately **no fuzzy string match** from the AI-extracted `material` free text to a part: a wrong auto-tie would deplete the wrong heat lot into an as-built record. **Amended 2026-08-10** — preview now computes a suggestion server-side (see [Automatic sheet-stock matching on nest preview](#automatic-sheet-stock-matching-on-nest-preview-2026-08-10)), and the amendment narrows what may become a tie rather than widening it: **nothing is ever derived from the AI-extracted material free text on the CLIENT. A tie is a planner pick, or a server-computed suggestion that cleared an exact-thickness gate, a real alloy agreement, an 8-point ambiguity margin and a sheet-form test — and that the planner then confirmed in the review grid before Import. The AI leg can only reorder a shortlist; it is structurally incapable of setting `auto_fill_part_id`.** This seam still sees only what a planner confirmed.

**The wizard pulls the OTHER way, and the direction is the whole safety argument** (2026-08-05). Picking a sheet part in the import wizard's review step now stamps that part's thickness and sheet size onto the nest row (`frontend/src/utils/sheetPart.ts` → `deriveSheetSpec`, parsed out of the shop's stocking convention `0.188-72X144-A36` / `10GA-72X120-CS` — a heuristic over `part_number` / `name`, deliberately not new `Part` columns and therefore no migration and no re-keying). That is **part → free text**, which is the safe direction: a human has named the stock, and the values land in editable fields that ship as nest metadata. The forbidden direction is unchanged on the client — nothing in the browser reads `material` / `thickness` back into a `material_part_id`, and the note that used to sit here ("do not 'complete the symmetry' later: the tie is what depletes a lot, so it stays a deliberate human pick") is **amended, not withdrawn, 2026-08-10**. The tie is still what depletes a lot. What the amendment permits is a *proposal* computed on the server under gates a client heuristic could never carry: **nothing is ever derived from the AI-extracted material free text on the CLIENT. A tie is a planner pick, or a server-computed suggestion that cleared an exact-thickness gate, a real alloy agreement, an 8-point ambiguity margin and a sheet-form test — and that the planner then confirmed in the review grid before Import. The AI leg can only reorder a shortlist; it is structurally incapable of setting `auto_fill_part_id`.** Three guards keep the pull-through honest and are not decoration — the row records the extractor value it displaced and restores it if the tie is cleared *and* the field is still untouched; a divergence from a non-empty extractor read renders amber, because that divergence is usually how a wrong part reveals itself; and the pull-through only fires for parts `isSheetLikePart` accepts, so tying a nest to non-sheet stock through the picker's "show all materials" escape hatch writes nothing rather than stamping angle-iron dimensions onto a sheet. The picker itself (`components/laser/SheetPartPicker.tsx`) always pins the current selection into its list even when the sheet-only filter would hide it — a picker that renders blank on a re-import is exactly how a work order gets silently untied.

### Automatic sheet-stock matching on nest preview (2026-08-10)

The planner picked the sheet by hand for every nest in a package — 42 combobox searches for one Miratech ZIP — and the measured consequence showed up the moment the ledger got a UI: **65 of 74 completed nests in July 2026 carried no tie at all**, and their sheet metal never left stock. The consumption engine's own record over the other 9 was perfect. The gap was never the engine; it was the toil in front of it. `backend/app/services/sheet_stock_matcher.py` (parsing in `sheet_stock_spec.py`, a Python port of the wizard's `frontend/src/utils/sheetPart.ts` plus scalar helpers) answers the question server-side so the review grid opens with the answer already in it.

**A suggestion is ADVISORY, and that is the whole product rule.** `auto_fill_part_id` is assigned only by the deterministic gate; the wizard pre-fills the picker from it and then treats it as a **proposal the planner confirms before Import**. The tie drives real inventory depletion at operation completion into an AS9100D as-built record that never auto-reverses, so nothing may commit a tie without one deliberate human act. Preview is also a **pure read** — no ledger row, no audit row, no event, no write of any kind, the same structural property `GET /parts/{id}/backflush-readiness` holds, and for the same reason: a preview is not an actor and records no intent.

The module **refuses far more readily than it picks**, and each refusal is a named policy:

- **Thickness is a HARD gate, ±0.002″ — not a score component.** No thickness agreement, no candidate, ever. The tolerance is derived, not chosen: the tightest adjacent pair in the gauge table is 24ga `0.0239` / 22ga `0.0299`, 0.006 apart, so 0.002 is 3× tighter than the tightest real gap and can never bridge two stocked gauges — while still absorbing the rounding between a nest reading `12ga` (0.1046) and a part numbered `0.105-72X144-CS`.
- **A stated alloy that disagrees DROPS the candidate.** There is **no string similarity anywhere in the matcher** — `rapidfuzz` is deliberately not imported. `A36` and `A572` are one edit apart and two different steels.
- **Alloy equivalence is SHOP POLICY encoded as a constant**, `ALLOY_EQUIVALENCE_SETS`, and it is the one place in the file where a wrong entry produces a wrong pre-fill on a fully-confident row. Two sets ship, both about carbon-steel naming: `{A36, CS, HRS, HR}` (A36 is the structural grade; the same rack gets called mild steel / carbon steel / hot rolled on a nest report) and `{CRS, CR}` (cold rolled). **304 and 316 are NOT in it, and neither are 5052 and 6061** — those substitutions are corrosion- and forming-relevant and are a metallurgist's call, not a matcher's. Leaving a real equivalence *out* fails soft: that descriptor degrades to a one-click shortlist instead of pre-filling. Adding a wrong one fails hard. Add entries only with a metallurgical argument, never to raise a pre-fill rate.
- **An under-specified alloy forces `ambiguous`.** A nest that says `SS` with both 304 and 316 in the rack, or states no grade at all, gets a shortlist and no pre-fill.
- **Two candidates within 8 points is not an answer.** `AUTO_FILL_MIN_MARGIN = 8.0`: two `0.250-60X120-A36` sheets from different suppliers both score 100.0 with a margin of 0, so the data does not identify one of them, neither is pre-filled, and both are shown. **This is the case that actually produces wrong-lot depletion**, and it is the case the margin rule exists for.

**The pre-fill bar.** Every survivor already cleared the thickness gate, so `score = 60 + 25×alloy + 15×size`, where alloy is 1.0 exact / 0.8 equivalence-set / 0.0 unknown, and size is 1.0 both dimensions / 0.6 one / 0.3 none stated / 0.0 conflict (**kept, not dropped** — a small nest is legitimately cut from a bigger sheet, and the shop does that). `AUTO_FILL_MIN_SCORE = 90.0` then falls out of the framing rather than being tuned to it: exact thickness + exact grade + exact size is 100.0, but a row whose **sheet size could not be read tops out at 89.5** — deliberately below the bar, because "read the sheet size and *then* pick" was what this was built to replace, and a row that produced no size did not do that. Pre-fill additionally requires the alloy agreement to be real (≥ 0.8, and the nest must have stated a grade) and the part to pass the sheet-form test, so dimension-matching angle iron can never be pre-filled onto a nest.

**On-hand annotates; it never ranks.** Ranking by "which lot happens to be full" would let a purchasing accident decide a heat lot, which is a manufacturing decision. A right-spec sheet with **zero on hand is still returned, still ranked first, and still pre-filled** — refusing to propose it ships the nest untied, which is exactly the failure this feature exists to close. What stock does contribute is a warning: on-hand is read through the engine's own `CONSUMABLE_ITEM_CLAUSES` (imported, never re-declared — the same discipline `material_tie_view` follows, so a number shown here can never promise stock the engine would refuse to draw), and the projection walks rows in **grid order** claiming stock as it goes, so twelve nests wanting eight sheets is visible at review time instead of at completion when the lot goes negative. The projection never re-ranks: row 30 must not get a different part than row 1 for the same spec, or one physical sheet splits across two part numbers in an as-built record.

**History demotes; it never promotes.** Prior planner ties to the same descriptor triple (365-day window, ≥ 3 ties, `cancelled` rows excluded because a re-import cancels and rebuilds wholesale) can force a row **out** of pre-fill and into review, and can lift a part the grammar cannot parse (`SHT-A36-STD`) into the shortlist. It can never promote anything **to** pre-fill: three repetitions of one mistake are indistinguishable from three correct decisions, and on a shop that has tied 9 nests in total it would be empty on day one anyway. The deterministic spec path carries the feature; history only ever adds doubt or context.

**The AI leg reorders a shortlist and nothing else.** For an `ambiguous` row `services/sheet_stock_ai_resolver.py` may ask Claude (task `sheet_stock_disambiguation`, per-task override `ANTHROPIC_SHEET_STOCK_MODEL`, pinned to the Default tier in the router rather than complexity-scored — a short shortlist prompt would score down to Haiku, and this is the metallurgy-and-size call the matcher *refused* to make; gated by the per-company `allow_ai_egress` kill switch like every other Anthropic call) to reorder the shortlist the deterministic gates already produced. It runs **after** the gate, may only re-order and re-word candidates the gate already admitted, leaves `score` exactly as the matcher computed it, stamps `basis="ai_disambiguated"` on what it lifted — and **never writes `auto_fill_part_id`**. That is what makes "structurally incapable" a property of the code rather than a promise: the field is assigned in one place, and the resolver is not it. A package the matcher resolved cleanly costs no call at all; a pick outside the shortlist, a pick with no reason, egress off, or any other failure leaves the deterministic order untouched with an advisory diagnostic (`AI_PICK_OUT_OF_SET` / `AI_UNAVAILABLE`) on the row.

**The single-nest manual modal deliberately does not get this.** `LaserNestManualModal` pre-fills nothing and asks for no suggestion: one nest at a time carries none of the per-row toil this addresses, and the planner is already looking at the one sheet they are describing. Spending an auto-fill's safety budget to save a single combobox search is a bad trade.

**Whether the suggestions were used is answerable afterwards.** The import takes an optional `sheet_match_provenance` form field — a closed four-value map (`auto` | `suggested` | `prefill` | `planner`) keyed by `source_file` — written into the WO-level audit row's `extra_data` and **omitted entirely when empty**, so an import from a client that never heard of suggestions writes byte-identical `extra_data`. It is observational: it never resolves anything and never creates a tie, and because it is untrusted client input landing in an append-only table it is bounded, vocabulary-filtered, and **discarded rather than 400'd** when malformed — refusing an import over a telemetry field would punish the planner for a wizard bug. Without it, "did the pre-fills get accepted, and were they right?" would have to be reconstructed from ties alone, which cannot distinguish a confirmed suggestion from a planner's own pick.

Full wire shapes — `SheetPartSuggestion` / `SheetPartCandidate` / `SheetMatchDiagnostic`, the three package counts, the diagnostic code vocabulary, and the `sheet_match_provenance` import field — are in `docs/API.md` → Laser Nests → "Sheet-stock suggestions on preview".

### Where a duplicate's ties come from (work-order duplicate, 2026-08)

`POST /api/v1/work-orders/{id}/duplicate` is the **third** server-side tie creator, alongside the two nest paths above, and it is the only one that creates a tie by reading an existing one. `services/work_order_duplicate_service.py::_copy_material_allocations` copies only `status == OPEN` ties — a `cancelled` / `closed` row is a tombstone (status **is** the tombstone here; there is no `is_deleted`), so a tie a planner untied or a nest re-import superseded is not part of the plan being re-run.

**Every copied tie lands inert, and one reset is load-bearing: the pins are ALWAYS cleared.** `pinned_inventory_item_id` / `pinned_lot_number` never carry across. A pin says *"consume from THIS lot"*, and the lot the source job pinned was very likely consumed **by** the source job — so the duplicate would either point at stock that no longer exists or, worse, at a row since re-received under a different heat that has nothing to do with this work. Unpinned means FIFO picks at consume time, which is the correct default for a job that has not started; it is the same posture the nest seam ships with. `qty_consumed` resets to 0 and `status` to `open` for the neighbouring reason — the ledger, not this row, is the record of what moved.

`qty_planned` is **recomputed, never copied**, because it is a number that was only ever true at the source's quantity: `qty_per_run × planned_runs` for a nest-backed operation tie (byte-identical to `create_nest_material_allocation`), `qty_per_run × quantity_ordered` for an ordinary operation tie, and the source value scaled by the **same** quantity ratio the copied plan hours took for a work-order-scoped tie — one factor for hours and material, or the duplicate would plan hours for one quantity and material for another. A same-quantity duplicate multiplies by exactly 1.0 and reproduces the source value bit-for-bit. `unit_of_measure` is re-snapshotted through the shared `_uom_value` helper rather than carried: the column documents itself as a snapshot of `Part.unit_of_measure` *at tie time*, and a part restocked in a different unit since the source job would otherwise hand the new tie a label its own quantity is no longer expressed in.

**Three conditions skip a tie rather than guess at it**, and a skip is reported to the planner, not just logged: `part_not_available` (the tied part has been soft-deleted — `POST …/material-allocations` refuses one outright, so re-creating it here would mint a tie nobody could have made by hand), `operation_not_copied` (its operation was skipped because that operation's laser nest was soft-deleted; re-scoping to the work order is not available, since a work-order-scoped tie carrying `qty_per_run` is a 422 on the tie API), and `nest_runs_unavailable` (nest-backed with no run count — substituting the work-order quantity would inflate one nest's sheet demand by roughly the nest count, since a laser WO's quantity is the **sum** of every nest's runs; nothing over-consumes on the back of `qty_planned`, but the shortage and MRP views read it). Each skip is written to the new work order's audit `extra_data` **and** returned in the duplicate's response envelope — the audit chain alone is not enough here, because the failure mode is a planner releasing a job that carries no demand for the material: no shortage shows, the nests run, and stock is never deducted until the count disagrees.

**Nothing about consumption changes.** A duplicate posts no ledger row, writes no `qty_consumed`, and fires no consumption leg — it creates planning rows only, and the new work order is a DRAFT that has completed nothing. Invariant 6(d) still holds byte-for-byte for a duplicate of an **untied** work order: no tie, no ledger row, no audit row, no event.

### Reading ties on the floor (PR 2)

`app/services/material_tie_view.py` is the single batched tie + on-hand read shared by the dispatch board and the kiosk queue, so the manager's chip and the operator's line can never disagree about what a tie means or how short it is. It is a **pure read with no write path** — a queue poll is not an actor, has no intent and records no reason, so it must never move stock — and it imports the engine's constants rather than restating them, precisely so the numbers shown to an operator cannot drift from the ones consumption acts on. **PR 4.4 took that one step further, and had to:** the module used to re-declare the consumable predicate verbatim, with a comment arguing the NULL-`status` exclusion was *"kept, not 'fixed': FIFO will not draw from such a lot either."* That rationale **inverted** the moment FIFO did draw from such a lot, so a re-declared copy would now **hide stock the engine will take**. It splats in `CONSUMABLE_ITEM_CLAUSES` instead — the module's own docstring already said it: *sharing the literal is the point; re-declaring it is the bug*.

Two rules it exists to enforce: it serves **open, operation-scoped ties only** (a work-order-scoped tie belongs to the whole job, so hanging it on cards would fan one tie across every card and read as N ties), and an untied operation gets **no key at all** in the result — not an empty list — so callers render nothing, no placeholder and no "not tied" nag. `on_hand` answers two different questions on purpose: a **pinned** tie is scored against that lot alone (pinning is lot-directed and an insufficient pinned lot is driven negative rather than spilled), while an **unpinned** tie is scored against the shared consumable predicate — the same clauses the draw uses, so the hint equals what consumption will actually take. The whole page costs at most three SELECTs regardless of card count.

### Returning consumed material (PR 3)

`POST /api/v1/work-orders/{id}/material-allocations/{allocation_id}/return` is the reasoned reversal, and the **first self-service path for every "reverse consumption first" refusal in the system**. Until it shipped, an over-consumed tie, a tie that had to give all its material back, and a `qty_planned` a planner needed to lower under posted consumption were all dead ends that the error copy could only apologize for.

It is the "compensating transaction + required reason + audit" pattern the receiving corrections established, applied to consumption. **Nothing historical is mutated**: each credit is an **appended** positive `TransactionType.RETURN` row. That enum member already existed and *nothing wrote it*, which is why adopting it disturbed no reader.

#### Two named intents, and nothing in between

This is the central design fact and the thing most likely to be "simplified" later.

| Intent | Bound | Tie afterwards |
|---|---|---|
| `correct_over_consumption` | `qty_consumed − live target` | stays **OPEN** |
| `return_and_untie` | the **full** `qty_consumed` (a mismatch is 422) | **CANCELLED**, same transaction |

Anything between the two is **422**, naming `return_and_untie` as the alternative.

**The bound on `correct_over_consumption` is not a safety hack bolted on top.** It is precisely the negative delta the sum-delta engine already computes and refuses to execute (invariant 6b), now performed by an actor with a reason. After it, `qty_consumed >= target`, so the engine no-ops forever — on every path, GETs included.

**Why the middle is closed rather than merely discouraged.** A return that leaves `qty_consumed` **below** the live target on a still-OPEN tie is not "a smaller return". `target` is recomputed from live operation state on every call, including from a **reconcile-on-read GET**, so that material is re-drawn on the very next completion *or page load* — and the re-draw re-runs FIFO, which can select a **different lot** than the material came from. One physical sheet would end up traced to two, fabricating heat/cert linkage in an as-built record (AS9100D 8.5.2). Leaving the tie under-consumed and OPEN is the one shape this verb must not be able to produce.

`target` is recomputed from the *exact* basis each consumption leg uses, so "the engine can no longer draw" means the same thing in both places: `qty_per_run × (complete + scrapped)` for an operation-scoped tie, `qty_planned` for a work-order-scoped one, and **0** for an operation-scoped tie whose operation is gone (detached by a re-import — no leg can reach it, so nothing is bounded). One consequence worth knowing: a work-order-scoped tie drained by the backflush sits at exactly `qty_consumed == qty_planned`, so its correction allowance is **zero** and `return_and_untie` is the only return available to it. That is correct — such a tie consumed precisely what it planned. An allowance opens only if the plan was later edited down. **Since PR 4.4 that identity is a fact rather than an assumption:** `_advance_tie_consumed` sets `qty_consumed` to the tie's *own signed ledger net*, re-read after posting, instead of writing `qty_planned` regardless of what landed (the plan's fourth residual). So `return_and_untie`'s "give back exactly `qty_consumed`" and `correct_over_consumption`'s `consumed − target` allowance are both exact by construction.

#### Material goes back to the lots it came off, or not at all

A return credits the **source lots**, walked **newest-first** (the reverse of how consumption posted — the most recent ISSUE is what an over-count correction compensates). A consumption that FIFO-spilled across three lots returns across those same three, so one logical return becomes N ledger rows and the response carries the per-lot breakdown: *"3 sheets to lot HT-4471, 1 sheet to lot HT-4470"*, never one anonymous total. **There is no lot to choose.** Crediting a convenient sink would invent heat/cert linkage, so the verb refuses instead — receiving's "409 rather than guess" posture. Two rows it will not credit: a **source lot that no longer exists** (nothing in `app/` deletes stock rows, so this means an out-of-band write) and a **placeholder stock row** (the lot-less, finished-goods-located anchor the engine mints when a part has no stock at all — crediting it would turn a ledger anchor into unlabeled, FIFO-eligible stock). Returning **into a negative lot is expected and not guarded**: a shortage-driven consumption drove it below zero and this unwinds it toward zero.

**Idempotency is arithmetic, not an index.** Nothing links a RETURN row to a specific ISSUE row and no unique index covers them, so double-crediting is prevented by capacity: per `(allocation_id, inventory_item_id)`, `issued − already-returned`. Prior returns are charged against the newest issue rows first — the same LIFO order the walk credits in — so the residual capacity lands on exactly the rows a prior return did not reach, and a replay cannot over-credit a lot however many times it runs. `GET …/{allocation_id}/consumption` returns that same per-lot arithmetic as a **pre-confirm read**, ordered identically, so the preview and the outcome cannot disagree.

Each RETURN row carries: the compensated row's `reference_type` / `reference_id` (**mirrored**, so `work_order_ledger_filter` — which matches on reference *shape* and never on transaction type — picks it up with **zero change to any reader**), the same `allocation_id`, `reason_code="MATERIAL_RETURN"`, the reason in the row's `notes` *and* the audit `description` *and* `extra_data.reason`, and the **compensated row's `unit_cost`**, never the lot's current one — a lot revaluation between consume and return would otherwise strand residual (or negative) material cost on the job.

#### What `qty_consumed` means now

It is still a **cache**; the ledger is still authoritative. What changed is that it is no longer monotonic-up: a return **lowers** it, clamping float dust to exactly 0 so a fully-returned tie reads `0` and not `4e-16` (otherwise the untie and hard-delete guards would read "material was consumed" against a tie that gave everything back). The authoritative consumed total in a compliance answer remains the **signed** sum of `inventory_transactions` carrying that `allocation_id` — ISSUE minus RETURN. The **three** guards that must never be fooled by the cache are keyed to the ledger for exactly this reason: hard delete (since PR 1), nest re-import (re-keyed in PR 3) and the manual untie (re-keyed in PR 4, the last one still reading the cache). Note the two guards read the ledger *differently*, on purpose: hard delete and re-import ask "would this orphan a ledger reference?", for which mere **existence** is right, since a RETURN row references the tie as durably as the ISSUE it compensates; untie asks "is material still out?", for which only the **signed** net is right.

#### Concurrency

A return writes neither the `WorkOrder` nor the `WorkOrderOperation` row, so **invariant 4's optimistic lock does not cover it** — unlike a completion, it takes no version conflict at all. The service therefore takes `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the **operation, then the work order** (the completion paths' and `production_reduction_service`'s order — two locks in two orders across two verbs is how deadlocks are built) *before* computing the bound, so a completion landing mid-request cannot raise `target` underneath the check and quietly invalidate the `correct_over_consumption` guarantee. Since 2026-07-31 the return also row-locks the **source stock rows** it credits (`_resolve_return_source_lots` fetches them `FOR UPDATE`, ascending-id — the shared lock-ordering rule): the credit is the same read-modify-write shape as the consume, so it takes the same row locks.

#### Every ledger reader now NETS returns — same PR, on the plan's own precedent

Lot genealogy was extended to read the new reference type in the PR that introduced it, on the rule that there is no release in which consumption exists but traceability cannot see it. The same obligation applies here, and it is sharper than it looks: **adding RETURN to a type filter without flipping the sign is worse than leaving it out**, because every one of these readers took `abs()` — a credit would have been counted as *more* consumption. The sign is keyed on `transaction_type`, never on the stored sign of the value, so a dataset with no RETURN rows is numerically **unchanged**.

| Reader | Effect |
|---|---|
| `traceability.py` → `_reconstruct_consumed_components` | The as-built record. Signed aggregation per `(WO, part, lot)`; a line that **nets to nothing is dropped entirely**, because a returned lot was never built into the part (AS9100D 8.5.2). The drop applies **only to keys that actually saw a RETURN**, so a job with no returns keeps every line it emitted before — including the degenerate zero-quantity line a zero-quantity ISSUE produces |
| `completion_cost_service._issued_material_cost` | `WorkOrder.actual_cost` / synced `JobCost`. **Not clamped** — a return is bounded by what the tie issued and priced off that ISSUE row, so a negative here would be real drift worth surfacing |
| `analytics_service` — `_issued_material_cost`, `_get_turnover_value`, per-part COGS | Shares one `_signed_cogs_cost()` CASE. The two **window-scoped** reads **are** clamped at 0: material issued before the window and returned inside it nets negative as a pure reporting-boundary artifact |
| `prediction_service._calculate_daily_usage` | Reorder points / MRP. Clamped at 0 for the same rolling-window reason. Material issued and then returned was never used — counting it as usage makes the shop re-buy material sitting on the rack |

The two cost services shared only the *ledger* predicate, not the *type* predicate — which is exactly how a docstring claiming they "cannot drift" could still be wrong — so the netting is applied in both, identically.

#### Reduce-production: the office verb now accepts a COMPLETE operation

`POST /work-orders/operations/{id}/reduce-production` passes `allow_completed_operation=True`; the operator's `POST /shop-floor/operations/{id}/reduce-production` does not, and **neither** accepts a terminal work order. Both gates run the same `_assert_reduction_in_scope` predicate before *and* under the row locks, so the fast-fail and the authoritative check cannot drift.

The refusal being relaxed was set on the rationale that a completed operation's downstream inventory / cost / FG effects had fired and could not be walked back. **The RETURN verb is that walk-back**, which is what makes the relaxation safe rather than merely convenient — and lowering a completed operation's count is precisely what opens the `correct_over_consumption` allowance the return is bounded by. Order matters on the floor: **reduce first** (the count is the record), then return the material the lower count no longer accounts for.

It also fixes a false referral. The operator's message — *"Completed work can't be corrected here -- ask a supervisor"* — was accurate about where to go and wrong about what would happen there: the supervisor's own endpoint hit the **identical** 409. The terminal-work-order refusal now carries its **own** message, deliberately split from that one, because pointing a caller at a supervisor for a terminal work order would be the same false referral in the other direction. Scrap fields and statuses are still never touched: a corrected COMPLETE operation stays COMPLETE, it just carries a truthful count.

### The BOM/routing backflush leg (PR 4)

**The leg is DARK, and PR 4 deliberately left it that way.** `Part.backflush_components` has **no writer anywhere in `app/`** — no schema field, no endpoint, no UI, `server_default="false"` — so `_resolve_backflush_components` and everything it calls have never executed against production data. The [Delivery](#delivery) row for PR 4 originally promised to *expose* the flag in this PR. Reading the leg first is what changed the plan: turning the flag on would have switched on a demand resolver whose output has never been produced, feeding a suppression check that has never executed, into an issue path exercised only with the single-part, tie-shaped demand PRs 1–3 built. **The owner re-scoped to harden it first — the same discipline PR 1 used when it shipped the engine dark.** Exposure moves to PR 4.5.

> **Status of that paragraph as of PR 4.5 (2026-07-27): the flag is exposed; the leg is still unproven.** "No writer anywhere in `app/`" is now historical — read it as *the state PR 4 hardened against*, not as current fact. See [Exposing the flag](#exposing-the-flag-pr-45). The rest of this section stands unrewritten and is still the description of what the leg does.

Read the state of that code as a licence to fix it *and* as a warning not to assume any of it was ever right. It shipped treating a whole-job component quantity as a per-unit rate, summing one component's demand once per operation that touched it, letting one routed operation cancel an entire BOM explosion, consuming both a sub-assembly and its raw material, and reading neither `is_alternate` nor `is_optional` nor reference lines.

#### What the leg means now (all of it flag-gated, none of it live)

- **Ledger-keyed suppression — the double-issue this document predicted in PR 1.** Suppression used to be status-keyed only (`_drop_allocation_covered_parts`, on `AllocationStatus.OPEN`). Three guards in a row miss the same case: a tie that **consumed** and is no longer `OPEN` is invisible to the status layer; `_component_already_issued` keys on `reference_type='work_order'` and therefore cannot see tied consumption at all (it posts under `work_order_operation`); and `uq_wo_inventory_issue`'s partial predicate does not cover those rows either — which is the whole reason the reference type was split. So the part left stock **twice**, and the as-built record carried two lines naming two **different lots** for one physical consumption (AS9100D 8.5.2). A second layer, `_drop_ledger_covered_parts`, now runs alongside the status one and keys on the **signed** ledger net, writing a tamper-evident **`BACKFLUSH_DOUBLE_ISSUE_BLOCKED`** row — the system declining to issue material a planner's BOM asked for is exactly the kind of control gap that must not be silent. Both layers are needed: the status layer suppresses an OPEN tie that has **not consumed yet** (the material is coming), which no ledger read can see.
- **It settles PR 3's open question by construction.** Because the net is signed, a **fully-returned** tie nets to zero and the backflush is free to issue that part again. That is correct rather than incidental: the material physically came back, the job holds none of it, and the BOM's demand is once again unmet. Suppressing on mere *existence* would leave the part permanently un-issuable on a job that genuinely returned the material, and would hide the gap from the shortage machinery that exists to surface it.
- **BOM line semantics.** `is_alternate`, `is_optional` and `reference` lines were read **nowhere** on this path, so enabling the flag would have issued every member of an alternate group (the group is an OR, not an AND — issuing all of them multiplies demand by the group's size) plus tooling and documentation lines. All three are now skipped. Under-issuing is the safe direction and is not silent: the material is still on the shelf, so the operator draws it manually and the job's cost shows the gap. Over-issuing writes material into a genealogy record that never contained it, which no downstream reader can distinguish from the truth.
- **Multi-level BOMs.** `phantom` is a planning fiction that is never stocked, so it explodes to its children at the phantom's extended quantity; `make` is a stocked unit that is issued while its subtree is **not** consumed (those children were consumed when it was built). Implemented in `_resolve_backflush_components`, deliberately **not** in `_collect_bom_components`, which has three other callers and whose flat output cannot express the distinction at all. A `make` subtree is still walked in exclude-only mode, so the routing leg cannot re-introduce through the back door the lines the explosion just declined to issue.
- **Routing precedence is now PER PART.** It used to be all-or-nothing (`if not required:`), so **one** stray `component_part_id` on **one** operation silently disabled the entire BOM explosion for the whole work order. That is not hypothetical: `_create_assembly_routing_operations` writes `component_part_id` only for components that *have* a released routing, so a ten-line BOM with two routed components lost the other eight. An operation naming a component states *that component's* demand and says nothing about the rest of the BOM. Routing wins for the parts it names; the BOM supplies the rest; disagreements are logged. A **self-consumption guard** was added alongside it — an operation naming the work order's own part would have ISSUEd the part the finished-goods leg just RECEIVEd.
- **Scrap is in the basis** — `quantity_complete + Σ(operation.quantity_scrapped)`. Previously a fully-scrapped work order backflushed **nothing**, and the same shop reported two different consumptions for the same physical event depending on whether the material happened to be tied.

  **The scrap term must come from OPERATION evidence, and a first attempt at this got it wrong** — recorded because the mistake is invisible on inspection and the wrong version was briefly written into the compliance changelog as a records-integrity fix. `WorkOrder.quantity_complete` **is** rolled up from operations (`sync_work_order_quantity_complete`, `max()`-guarded and monotonic-up). `WorkOrder.quantity_scrapped` **is not rolled up at all** — its only writers in `app/` are a child reset, a null-guard, force-complete's explicit override, and the manual office edit. So a basis of `work_order.quantity_complete + work_order.quantity_scrapped` reads scrap as **zero** for the ordinary case: an operator who scraps 3 of 10 at the kiosk leaves `operation.quantity_scrapped = 3` and the work-order column at `0`. The tie engine would consume for 10 and the backflush for 7 — *precisely the divergence the change claims to close*. Summing across operations is right rather than double-counting: a unit scrapped at op 10 and a unit scrapped at op 20 are different units, and both consumed the job's material. `work_order.quantity_scrapped` is used only as a fallback when no operation carries scrap, which is exactly the force-complete-with-explicit-override path that sets it.

> **Honesty note on "the BOM alternates fix".** The Delivery row's original phrasing oversells what shipped. Alternates remain **two inert columns with no substitution logic** — nothing selects a primary, nothing substitutes, nothing records which member was used. What PR 4 did is make two BOM-explosion helpers **agree about what a line means**: `mrp_service` has always skipped alternates (`explode_bom_for_mrp`), and now the backflush does too, so planning and consumption cannot state different demand for one BOM. That is worth having and it is not the alternates feature.

#### Two quantity defects nobody predicted — the strongest evidence for hardening dark

Both were catastrophic on exposure, and neither appeared on any risk list before the code was read:

- **`component_quantity` is the WHOLE-JOB total, not a per-unit rate.** Both writers store `qty_per_assembly × work_order.quantity_ordered`, and the leg multiplied it by the produced quantity **again**. A 100-piece job at 2 per unit would have demanded **20,000**. The rate is now recovered by dividing back out by `quantity_ordered` and re-scaling, so the routing and the BOM answer in the same units.
- **One component's demand is replicated across every operation that touches it, and the leg SUMMED them.** A component with a three-operation routing tripled its own demand. Reduced with `max` — the stated value when the rows agree, incapable of multiplying by the operation count, and (unlike `min`) it does not silently under-issue to 0 when one row was never reconciled.

#### The one live behavior change

**The manual-untie guard moved from the `qty_consumed` cache to the signed ledger net.** This closes an asymmetry PR 3 left standing: hard delete has read the ledger since PR 1 and nest re-import since PR 3, while untie still read the cache — an asymmetry nobody chose. The cache's own documented semantics make it unsafe here, in **both** directions: a `correct_over_consumption` down to a zero live target leaves `qty_consumed` at 0 on a still-OPEN tie the ledger may still be backing (the cache permitted an untie it should have refused), while the backflush advances a work-order-scoped tie's `qty_consumed` to `qty_planned` — which is not what the ISSUE posted — so the cache could refuse an untie that would have stranded nothing.

**Signed net rather than existence**, deliberately: existence-keying would dead-end a fully-returned tie, 409-ing forever while `return_and_untie` 422s with nothing left to return. `_component_already_issued` stays **existence**-keyed for the opposite and equally deliberate reason — see the third residual below.

#### Residuals PR 4 recorded — three RESOLVED by PR 4.4, one permanent for legacy work orders

Each is kept with its original statement rather than deleted: what the leg looked like when it was hardened dark, and what changed, are both part of the record. See [The reconciling backflush](#the-reconciling-backflush-and-one-lot-policy-pr-44) for the mechanism.

- ~~**Two contradictory lot-selection policies remain.**~~ **RESOLVED (PR 4.4).** The residual read: *the backflush picks **lowest-`id`**, ignores `status`, ignores `location`, uses a **single** lot and drives it negative; the per-run tie engine walks **`received_date` FIFO**, filters `status='available'`, and spills across lots — and **both write lot genealogy**, so on the same material they can name different heats for the same physical draw.* Both engines now share `consumable_source_items` (`received_date ASC NULLS LAST, id ASC` over `CONSUMABLE_ITEM_CLAUSES`) and `plan_stock_draw`. The reason PR 4 declined it — `_issue_one_component` is **LIVE**, so changing which lot it picks changes shipped genealogy, and `status='available'` would newly exclude legacy NULL-status rows — was correct and is exactly what PR 4.4 had to answer: the predicate is `COALESCE(status, 'available') = 'available'`, a strict widening that keeps NULL-status stock eligible, and the change is stated as a live behaviour change with a prod blast-radius query attached rather than landed quietly. `location` is still not a selection input on either engine (unchanged, and not claimed as fixed).
- ~~**The backflush is a one-shot, not a reconciler.**~~ **RESOLVED for the arithmetic (PR 4.4) — and NOT for the trigger, which is the part that matters most to read correctly.** The residual read: *`uq_wo_inventory_issue` permits one ISSUE per (work order, part) **forever**, so a later raise to `quantity_complete` never issues the increment.* The leg is now delta-keyed per demand source (`delta = target − signed ledger net`), posts under `work_order_backflush` outside that index, and converges on replay instead of being fenced by an existence check. **But PR 4.4 adds no re-entry trigger, so "a later rise in `quantity_complete` issues nothing" REMAINS TRUE** — the leg still runs exactly once per work-order lifetime. The arithmetic is fixed; the trigger is not added. See the PR 4.4 [delivery row](#delivery) for why, and for what would have to be decided before one is.
- **A work-order-scoped `return_and_untie` leaves the part permanently un-issuable on that work order** — **RESOLVED for work orders created after PR 4.4; PERMANENT for legacy ones.** The residual claimed this was *"not choosable in code"*: netting `_component_already_issued` would attempt a re-issue, lose to `uq_wo_inventory_issue`, and have the loss swallowed as a duplicate no-op — claiming a consumption that never posted. That reasoning was sound about the shape it described and is retired with it: the leg's rows no longer occupy that index's slot, it reconciles against a **signed** net, and a re-issue would post cleanly. **For a work order carrying a legacy pre-4.4 `('work_order', ISSUE)` row the residual stands, forever** — the row survives the return, `_component_already_issued` keeps firing on it, and no backfill will ever re-key it (see [Compliance notes](#compliance-notes): nothing rewrites a posted ledger row). The remedy in that case is unchanged: tie at the operation level, which posts outside the index. **Be precise about what "resolved" buys today:** it removes the *permanent* block, not the absence of a trigger — nothing calls the leg again after a return, and the state is unreachable on a still-OPEN tie in any case (`return_and_untie` CANCELs the tie in the same transaction, and `correct_over_consumption` is bounded by `qty_consumed − qty_planned`, which is ≤ 0 for a fully-drained work-order-scoped tie).
- ~~**A work-order-scoped tie's `qty_consumed` can sit BELOW the ledger net.**~~ **RESOLVED (PR 4.4).** The residual read: *`_mark_work_order_ties_consumed` advances it to `qty_planned`, while the ISSUE row carries the **summed** BOM + tie demand under that tie's `allocation_id`, so a `return_and_untie` may be unable to drive the ledger to zero and the untie 409 stands.* Both halves are gone: the summing is deleted (BOM and tie post separate, separately-attributed rows), and `_advance_tie_consumed` writes the tie's **own signed ledger net**, re-read after posting. ### The reconciling backflush and one lot policy (PR 4.4)

**A third ledger reference shape, and no migration.** Reconciled component consumption posts `reference_type='work_order_backflush'` with `reference_id` = the **work order** — deliberately outside `uq_wo_inventory_issue`, because reconcile-to-target is not expressible under a one-row-per-(company, WO, part) index: the leg spills across as many lots as the demand needs and must be able to post a later top-up row. `reference_type` is unconstrained `String(50)` free text with no CHECK, enum or domain, and `work_order_operation` was introduced the same way by PR 1, so **no Alembic revision is needed and none is added** (head stays `076_uq_wo_inv_sqlite_parity`; `uq_wo_inventory_receipt` and `uq_wo_inventory_issue` keep their exact definitions on both dialects). **Why a reference-shape split rather than dropping the index.** Dropping `uq_wo_inventory_issue` would have bought reconciliation at the price of a production DDL on the hottest ledger table whose **downgrade becomes un-runnable the moment the first spill row exists** — it would have to `CREATE UNIQUE INDEX` over data that legitimately violates it. The split gets the same two goals with zero DDL, no lock, no deploy window, and a rollback that is a plain code revert. It is also safer for *history*: `_component_already_issued`, kept verbatim on `reference_type='work_order'`, now matches **only** pre-4.4 rows and fences those work orders out of the new engine entirely — including the summed rows that carry a non-NULL `allocation_id`, against which a net-based guard would have read zero for the BOM portion and re-issued it. That class of bug becomes structurally impossible rather than arithmetically avoided. It is consulted on **both** legs.

**Idempotency is arithmetic, and it is only valid because the writes are serialized.** No index covers the new shape. Per demand source, `delta = target − signed ledger net`, posted only when `delta > ε`; a non-positive delta is a **silent no-op, never an auto-reversal** (invariant 6b — this path runs from a GET with no actor and no reason). The two nets are disjoint and complete: the BOM net filters `allocation_id IS NULL`, and every tie-driven row carries one. Serialization is pre-existing, not new: the four write entries already hold `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the `work_orders` row before the inventory legs, and the two reconcile-on-read entries always UPDATE that same `version_id_col`-mapped row, so a loser raises `StaleDataError` and its whole reconcile rolls back. **No new `FOR UPDATE` was taken on the `work_orders` row** — at the time of writing it would have been the codebase's first on a GET, inside a loop guarded by `except Exception: pass`, which cannot rescue a lock *wait*. (**Superseded in part, 2026-07-31:** the engine now does take `FOR UPDATE` on the **inventory rows** it decrements — on every path, the GET-reached reconcile included — because the cross-work-order lot race is real regardless of which verb reached the engine; see "Concurrency is carried by TWO locks" above. The emit site is unchanged.

> **What was wrong.** `schemas/bom.py` defaulted a BOM line's `unit_of_measure` to the literal **`"each"`**, so a line created without an explicit UoM — which is most of them, through the UI, the API and every importer; *neither* BOM form in the frontend even sends the field — stated `each` regardless of how the component is actually stocked. `unit_of_measure_mismatch` is **`blocking`**, so on a sheet-metal shop's data (components in **sheets / lbs / ft / in**) `PUT /parts/{id}` returned **409** for almost every part, and an already-armed part refused those components at **completion** too ([item 2](#what-pr-45-does-not-do--read-this-literally)) — no material moves, one `BACKFLUSH_DEMAND_REFUSED` row per condition, one `material.backflush_demand_refused` notification per refused component. The resolution happens where the component part is loadable — `api/endpoints/bom.py` → `_resolve_line_uom`, applied on **all four** BOM-line write paths (single add, BOM-with-lines create, and both importers) plus the update path when a caller clears the field — never in the schema, which cannot see the part it would be defaulting for. `BOMItemBase.unit_of_measure` is now `Optional[str] = None`, meaning *"resolve this from the component"*, and a stated value always wins.

Nothing in production has opted in; the column's `server_default` is still `false`.

**Where the field lives, and why that is the whole security design.** It is on **`PartResponse` and `PartUpdate` only**. It is deliberately **not** on `PartBase`, and therefore not on `PartCreate`, because `PartCreate` is a bare subclass of `PartBase` and **both** create endpoints splat `Part(**data)` — as do both CSV importers. A field on `PartBase` would have become settable on four write paths at once, with no gate and no readiness check, turning a shop-wide "consume this part's BOM automatically, forever" policy into something a spreadsheet column could switch on. A part is therefore always **created off** and can only be switched on through an update. An explicit `null` is **422** rather than an `IntegrityError` on a `NOT NULL` column.

**One gate, two doors.** `PUT /parts/{id}` and `PUT /materials/{id}` are byte-identical `setattr` loops over the same `PartUpdate` schema writing the same `parts` rows, so the gate is defined **once** (`assert_backflush_change_allowed` in `parts.py`) and **imported** by `materials.py`, exactly as `_part_to_response` already was. A gate implemented in only one of the two files is not a gate. It runs **before the first `setattr`**, so a refusal leaves the row untouched.

- **Turning it ON** runs `backflush_readiness_for_part` and refuses **409** if any diagnostic is `blocking`. `detail` is a **plain string** — the blocker sentences joined — because the Axios interceptor renders a server `detail` verbatim and the point of the refusal is that a human reads it and goes and fixes a BOM line. One template for every sentence: *"Part {part_number} cannot enable automatic backflush: {what is wrong, naming the BOM line or operation}. {what to change}."*
- **Turning it OFF is never gated.** Stopping automatic consumption cannot issue wrong material.
- **Re-stating the current value is not a state change** and is not gated — otherwise a client PUTting a whole form back would be refused for a BOM defect it is not introducing.
- **The audit row carries the verdict, not just the value.** The flip itself already lands in `log_update`'s `changes` map; `extra_data` adds `backflush_readiness` (`clean` / `not_evaluated_disable`), `backflush_readiness_checked_at`, and the advisory codes that stood at the time. That verdict is **not reconstructable afterwards** once the BOM has been edited, which is the only reason it is recorded. **`clean` asserts less than it looks like it does, and the limit belongs beside the value:** it means *no blocking diagnostic in this part's own BOM explosion, at that instant*. The **routing half is not checked at opt-in at all** (its four codes need a work order), nothing re-validates between the flip and any later completion, and every input stays editable by anyone with `boms:edit` / `routings:edit`. The BOM edit path at least now *says so* — it warns and stamps `extra_data.backflush_armed_parts` on its own audit row, which is what makes the flip and the later edit correlatable on one chain ([below](#the-bom-edit-path-is-no-longer-blind)); the routing edit path is still silent. See "What PR 4.5 does NOT do" items 2 and 3.
- **Both doors log the flip as `resource_type='part'`, so the arming trail is ONE query.** `PUT /materials/{id}` writes the same `parts` rows and was normalised from `"material"` to `"part"` for exactly this reason (`create_material` / `delete_material` still log `"material"`). The canonical audit query recipe — including how to narrow it to arming rather than disarming, and what it does and does not return — lives in `docs/CMMC_LEVEL_2_COMPLIANCE.md`'s 2026-07-27 (PR 4.5) changelog row, item (3). It is deliberately written down in exactly one place.

**Two pure reads, and their purity is structural.** `GET /parts/{id}/backflush-readiness` and `GET /work-orders/{id}/backflush-preview` write **nothing** — no ledger row, no `audit_log` row (in particular no `BACKFLUSH_DOUBLE_ISSUE_BLOCKED`), no operational event. That is not discipline: the resolution layer takes no `AuditService` at all, and the recording layer is a separate function only the completion path calls. This is the same boundary `material_tie_view.py` exists to hold — a poll is not an actor and records no reason — and the preview was deliberately **not** built by savepoint-and-rollback, which would have coupled a read to a write path forever for the sake of one audit row.

**The preview models the ISSUE LOOP, not the demand resolver.** Both legs in the real order (work-order-scoped ties first, so a tie's pin gets first claim on stock), the legacy `('work_order', ISSUE)` fence, the reconcile-to-target delta, and the actual lot pick through the same `consumable_source_items` + `plan_stock_draw` the writer uses. A preview built on its own predicate would name a heat the backflush will never draw and hide one it will — silently, and only discoverable after material moved.

**A diagnostics layer — additive to the resolver, but ACTED ON by the completion path.** The resolver's warn-and-continue and silently-`continue` sites now *also* append a typed `_BackflushDiagnostic` (`code`, `severity`, `detail`, plus optional BOM-item / component / operation context). Every existing log line stays where it was and **the pure resolver returns exactly what it returned before** — acting on a diagnostic is the caller's job, and only the completion-path wrapper `_resolve_backflush_components` does it. **Twenty-one codes today: 16 present as `blocking`, 6 as `advisory`** — `no_demand_source` is both (advisory at work-order scope, blocking at part opt-in) and `deleted_part` exists only at part scope. A **BOM depth cap of 20** was added mirroring `explode_bom_recursive`: the visited-set guard bounds repetition of a *part*, not recursion depth, and a `RecursionError` here would be swallowed whole by the `except Exception: pass` at the two reconcile-on-read call sites — losing the entire completion's inventory effects, FG receipt included, to a log line.

**Readiness is a snapshot, never authorization — and it is a snapshot of the BOM half only.** Every input it reads — BOM lines and their `is_alternate` / `is_optional` / `item_type` / `quantity` / `unit_of_measure` — is mutable afterwards by other people, so the identical check re-runs server-side on the write. It runs the explosion at a **synthetic basis of 1.0**, because the real basis is `quantity_complete + operation scrap`, which is zero at opt-in time and would pronounce **every** part clean.

**Three limits on `eligible: true`, stated together because they compound.** (i) `backflush_readiness_for_part` runs the BOM explosion and **nothing else** — the routing is never examined at part scope, so the four routing codes cannot appear and `eligible: true` says nothing about them. (ii) The check happens **once**, at the flip; nothing re-runs it on a BOM edit, a routing change, a release or a completion. (iii) Every input stays editable by the **same ADMIN/MANAGER/SUPERVISOR tier** through `boms:edit` / `routings:edit`. When this was written **nothing on either edit path knew the part was armed** — no warning, no re-check, no audit annotation, no notification. The BOM half of that has since been closed by the BOM-line audit change ([below](#the-bom-edit-path-is-no-longer-blind)): a BOM-line write now **warns** and **annotates its audit row**. Still absent, deliberately or otherwise: **no re-check** (the readiness explosion is not re-run on an edit), **no notification** on the editing side, and **nothing at all on the ROUTING edit path**. The gate therefore still protects the **instant of the flip and nothing after it**; what changed is that the edit is now visible on the chain, not that it is validated. What stands behind it is the completion-time refusal (item 2 below), which is a **net**, not a second gate: it catches the BOM conditions the resolver can detect on the job in front of it and records each one, and it does not restore the assurance a reader takes from a recorded `clean` verdict.

### The BOM edit path is no longer blind

**Shipped after PR 4.5, in the BOM-line audit change.** Three statements above were written when
the BOM edit path had no idea a part was armed. That is now half-closed, and the half matters:

- **What it does.** `POST /bom/{id}/items`, `PUT /bom/items/{id}` and `DELETE /bom/items/{id}`
  each resolve the armed parts whose demand the edited BOM helps state — the BOM's own part, or
  an ancestor that reaches it through any line the explosion descends into, i.e. anything
  **except `buy`** — and, when the list is non-empty, return a `backflush_armed_warning`
  sentence and stamp `extra_data.backflush_armed_parts` on the `bom_line` audit row. That
  annotation is the point: it is the edit-side counterpart of the arming row's
  `extra_data.backflush_readiness`, so an assessor can put "who armed this, on what verdict"
  and "who edited the BOM afterwards" on one chain. Before this, the second half did not exist
  as a record at all.
- **`make` lines ARE followed, and getting this wrong was a real false negative.** The first
  version of this walk stopped at `make`, reasoning that a `make` sub-assembly is issued as a
  stocked unit and its children never are, so an armed grandparent could not be affected. That
  is true of the BOM **demand** leg and **false** of the leg beside it. `_explode_backflush_bom`
  still walks a `make` subtree in exclude-only mode, and every line it passes there lands in
  `excluded_part_ids` — which is load-bearing: a routing operation naming a part in that set
  raises `routing_component_excluded_by_bom` at **`blocking`** severity. So adding a line under
  a `make` sub-assembly can newly **block** an armed ancestor's routing demand, and deleting one
  can newly **un-block** it and let material issue that was previously refused — silently, on a
  hard-deleted row, with no warning. `bom_depth_exceeded` is a second such path: it is raised
  *before* the `consumed` check, so deepening a `make` subtree past `_MAX_BOM_LEVELS` refuses
  the armed ancestor's **whole** leg.

  **`buy` is the only wall**, and it is a real one: the explosion gates its child-BOM lookup on
  `item_type != 'buy'`, so nothing under a `buy` line is read at all — no demand, no exclusion,
  no diagnostic. The ascent's predicate mirrors that exact test (`COALESCE(item_type,'') !=
  'buy'`) rather than naming line types, so an empty or unrecognised `item_type` is ascended
  through here exactly as the explosion descends into it. Both directions are pinned by test.
- **It WARNS; it does not REFUSE, and that is the plan's own posture, not a shortcut.** A 409
  would be exactly the second gate this document declined to build, it would be a live
  behaviour change on the engineering-edit path, and it would **block its own remedy** — the
  documented fix for a blocking unit mismatch is `PUT /bom/items/{id}`, which an armed-part
  refusal would make impossible without first disarming (itself a gated flip). The
  completion-time `BACKFLUSH_DEMAND_REFUSED` remains the net.
- **What it still does NOT do.** It does not re-run `backflush_readiness_for_part` (that walks
  the whole BOM; putting it on every line write would cost real time in the BOM tab's Batch Add
  loop *and* make the write path depend on the resolution layer's verdict — the coupling the
  no-`AuditService` rule exists to prevent). It emits **no** notification. It says nothing about
  the **routing** edit path, which is still entirely silent. And the resolver it uses stops
  ascending at the first level containing an armed part, so the list reads "**at least** these",
  not a complete ancestor census.
- **Cost, because it lands in a hot loop — measured, not asserted.** The BOM tab's Batch Add
  issues one request per line (~40 for a real assembly), so the walk is shaped to be cheap
  rather than merely bounded: its **first level is the pre-check**. Counting SQL statements
  (`test_the_walk_costs_two_queries_on_an_ordinary_batch_add_line`):

  | shape | queries |
  |---|---|
  | line added to a top-level assembly's own BOM (**the Batch Add case**) | **2** |
  | part one `make`/`phantom` level down | 3 |
  | part two levels down | 4 |
  | immediate parent armed (short-circuit) | 2 |

  So: one PK read of the BOM's own part, then one parent probe per ancestor **level** — and
  for a top-level assembly that first probe returns no rows and ends the walk. Widening the
  ascent from phantom-only to "anything but `buy`" did not change the Batch Add number,
  because a top-level assembly is not a component of anything. Cost is bounded by
  `_MAX_BOM_LEVELS` and the visited set, and the ascent stops at the first armed level.

**Client surface.** A `PartBackflushCard` on PartDetail (current state, readiness, blocker list, a confirm modal, non-optimistic), a `BackflushPreviewPanel` on WorkOrderDetail, one narrow `setPartBackflush` client method (the flag is kept off the client `PartUpdate` type, mirroring the server), a `/parts` ETag invalidation the tie-cache helper never covered, and the **per-operation tie editor** that PR 4 moved here — hard-scoped to the operation, gated on `work_orders:edit`, with its own refresh token into `MaterialTiesPanel` because a tie write does not bump `work_orders.updated_at`.

**The one client gap PR 4.5 left, and its follow-up.** PR 4.5 shipped `GET /bom/uom-mismatches` **API-only**, so the arming *prerequisite* was the one part of the flow with no screen — the gate was reachable but the remediation worklist behind it was not workable by anyone in the shop. The follow-up added the **BOM Unit Mismatches** page at **`/bom/uom-mismatches`** (sidebar: Engineering → *BOM Unit Mismatches*, directly after *Bill of Materials*), gated on `boms:edit` = **ADMIN / MANAGER / SUPERVISOR** on both the route and the nav entry, matching the endpoint. Frontend: `pages/BOMUomMismatches.tsx`, `api.getBOMUomMismatches`, and the `BOMUomMismatchReport` / `BOMLineUomMismatch` / `BOMUomMismatchParams` types. It is **read-only** — server-paged, filters in URL params, every row deep-linking to `/bom?id={bom_id}` and to the part's own readiness — because BOM-line writes emitted no audit rows at the time (that blocker has since been closed — see [The BOM edit path is no longer blind](#the-bom-edit-path-is-no-longer-blind) — so an inline editor is no longer refused on compliance grounds, merely unbuilt), and it renders `truncated` as a floor (`≥ N` plus a banner) and `blocks_backflush` as **"Line effect"** rather than as a per-part verdict. Both of those are the screen refusing to overstate what the report knows. It also required one small nav mechanism: `NavItem` gained an optional `permission`, and `visibleNavigation` filters items **and** group children by it, dropping a group left with no visible children; existing items carry no `permission` and are unaffected.

#### What PR 4.5 does NOT do — read this literally

**This series has now shipped THREE delivery rows that needed an honesty note** (PR 4's re-scope, PR 2.5's incremental-depletion correction, and PR 4.4's "arithmetic, not trigger"). This is the guard against a fourth.

1. **It adds no re-entry trigger. PR 4.4's item 1 stays true, word for word.** A later rise in `quantity_complete` after the work order has completed still issues **nothing** — the leg is not suppressed, it is **not re-entered at all**, so there is not even an audit row explaining the gap. Exposure changes only who can observe that: it was a property of dark code, and it is now a production behaviour waiting for the first opted-in part. 2. ~~A blocking diagnostic still does not refuse anything at COMPLETION.~~ **Corrected under review — it does, and this is the one item in this list that moved.** The first draft deferred the refusal to "its own PR" on the argument that it is a behaviour change to the leg. #### Found under review and CLOSED before merge

**The compliance audit of this branch returned FAIL** (two gating findings) and the code review returned eight findings. Everything below was fixed on the branch before merge. The four that follow are the ones created or made live *by exposure itself*, recorded at length because each was a residual somebody could otherwise have carried forward as accepted:

- **Cross-tenant disclosure through a diagnostic (the only security defect).** `BOMItem.component_part` was joinedloaded with no `company_id` predicate, and `bom.py`'s add-line validator resolves `component_part_id` unscoped — so a BOM line pointing at another company's part is reachable through supported verbs. Harmless while nothing rendered the object; a disclosure the moment the readiness GET (open to **every** authenticated tenant user) and the 409 started serving diagnostic sentences. Fixed at the **lookup** (`_tenant_components`), so the foreign row is never materialised: an unresolvable id yields `missing_component_part` carrying **only `bom_item_id`**, while a same-tenant soft-deleted component still names itself. `bom.py:1497`'s unscoped validator remains a separate open defect.
- **Blocking diagnostics computed on the write path and discarded** — see item 2 above.
- **A soft-deleted part could be armed, and would move stock.** Neither PUT lookup filters `is_deleted`, and `delete_part` checks dependencies only on a hard delete, so a soft-deleted part keeps its in-flight work orders. Closed with a blocking `deleted_part` diagnostic inside `backflush_readiness_for_part`, which shuts both doors and the read without touching a lookup four other handlers share.
- **Preview/outcome disagreements.** The preview listed only `plan_stock_draw`'s covered takes and reported the remainder as a scalar, while the writer posts it as a second ISSUE against a **named** lot (25 over lots of 10 and 5 previewed "short 10" and posted B twice, B ending at −10). Both now share `_shortfall_anchor`, and the remainder appears as a flagged lot row (`is_shortfall`) or `shortfall_creates_placeholder`. A tie whose pinned lot was **held after pinning** also previewed clean while the completion consumes it; now `pinned_lot_is_held`.

Five more, smaller, from the same reviews and also closed on the branch: **`missing_component_part` / `circular_bom` were blocking on lines the leg never issues** (they were emitted before `line_consumed` was computed, so a `reference` line pointing at a hard-deleted fixture refused the opt-in forever over demand of zero) — both are now gated on `line_consumed` like the other per-line diagnostics; **`no_demand_source` was blocking on the work-order preview**, painting a red banner over every healthy job whose part simply has no BOM — demoted to advisory at work-order scope, blocking only at part opt-in; **two dead code paths carrying false rationales** (`_resolve_backflush_demand`'s `basis_override`, which claimed a caller that could not use it, and `backflush_enable_blockers`, which had none) removed; **`PartBackflushCard` rendered on every part**, so a purchased part or raw material showed a permanent red "cannot be enabled" block on a page where the feature is meaningless — now gated by `showsBackflushCard` on part type (or on the flag already being on); and **three in-code docstrings still asserting the leg is dark** — the module docstring's *"read that as a licence to fix them"*, `backflush_components_for_work_order`'s, and `tests/api/test_backflush_breadth.py`'s — corrected, because that licence expired with this commit.

### Deliberately deferred

- **Reservations.** Bringing `quantity_allocated` to life changes `quantity_available` platform-wide — the receipt-void guard would start refusing voids for merely-tied stock — and makes MRP double-count, since it nets `on_hand − allocated` while separately exploding the same work-order demand. When it lands, MRP tied-demand deduplication and a re-audit of every `quantity_available` guard are in-scope requirements of that same PR.
- **Unit conversion.** Material is consumed in the part's own unit of measure, snapshotted onto the tie at creation. Nothing in the platform converts units. Because the tie's UoM is always the part's own, the only way to state a cross-UOM intent is a **lot pin naming a different part** — refused with **422** (the detail names the UoM clash when the two parts' units also differ) rather than guessed at.
- **Reversal — ~~deferred~~ SHIPPED in PR 3**, and kept here rather than deleted because this entry made a promise that PR 3 deliberately did **not** keep. It read: *"a laser work order carrying consumed ties cannot have its nest package re-imported at all until PR 3 ships."* That is **wrong**, and the owner chose to correct the claim rather than build to it. The RETURN verb exists and is the self-service path for every other "reverse consumption first" refusal ([Returning consumed material](#returning-consumed-material-pr-3)) — but re-import, hard delete, and `_component_already_issued`'s work-order-scoped 409 all key on the **existence** of ledger rows, and a return **appends** a row rather than removing one. All three still refuse after a full return, correctly. (Since PR 4.4 the third of those is a **legacy fence only** — it matches no row this engine writes — and it is unreachable; the backflush's own reconciliation is signed, so a returned quantity *is* unmet demand again on a post-4.4 work order.) **The manual untie is the one exception, and only since PR 4**, which keyed it to the *signed* net rather than existence — so a fully-returned tie can be untied. That is a different question ("is material still out?"), not an inconsistency; do not generalize it to the other three. Opening a new work order is still the answer for a locked nest package; see the fourth [residual gap](#residual-gaps-of-the-operation-completion-trigger) for the structural fix (supersede operations instead of deleting them) that would actually close it.
- **Per-run consumption.** PR 2.5 landed the per-*operation* trigger ([Capability vs. wiring](#capability-vs-wiring--when-consumption-actually-fires)); per-*run* depletion — stock moving as an operator reports 3 of 6 runs on a still-open nest — is still not built and production reporting is deliberately not a consumption trigger. This entry used to say it "should follow PR 3's RETURN verb". **PR 3 has landed, and it removes the stated blocker without making per-run consumption a decided thing.** What was missing was a safe walk-back story for an operation reporting partial production, which is still `IN_PROGRESS` and therefore still reducible; the return verb is that story. What is now missing is narrower and unresolved: the reduce-production relaxation went to the **office** verb only, so an operator reducing their own count on an in-progress operation would still strand material that only a supervisor can hand back. Per-run consumption should not land until that asymmetry is answered — either by extending the return to the shop floor (a stock-moving power the kiosk path fence currently, deliberately, keeps out) or by accepting supervisor-mediated correction as the workflow.
- **Buy-to-job**, remnant/offcut intake, kitting, and operator lot-picking at the kiosk. Shapes are recorded; none are built.

## Compliance notes

- Every consumption, tie, edit, untie, and lifecycle cancellation writes tamper-evident audit rows through `AuditService` — including the stock decrement each consumption causes, the `qty_consumed` advance on **both** tie shapes (the work-order-scoped drain is audited by the backflush leg, symmetric with the per-run engine), the `ALLOCATION_SHORTAGE` row when a lot is driven negative, the `HELD_MATERIAL_CONSUMED` row when a pinned lot was held after pinning, and the `ALLOCATION_CONSUMPTION_FAILED` row when an allocation's consumption is rolled back. `audit` is a **required** parameter on the lifecycle cancel helpers and on the nest rebuild — never an optional one that defaults to `None`. Historical transactions are never mutated. **Reversal landed in PR 3 as promised in shape**: an appended, signed, compensating `RETURN` row — the pattern established by receiving corrections — never an edit. The engine itself still refuses to auto-reverse; a return requires an actor, a non-blank reason (validated at the Pydantic boundary, so a blank one is FastAPI's own 422 rather than a hand-rolled refusal downstream) and writes that reason in **three** places an auditor might pull: the ledger row's `notes`, the audit `description`, and `extra_data.reason`. The receiving void path put it in only one, and a reason that is not on the record an auditor pulled is a reason nobody reads. A `return_and_untie` writes a **second** chain row for the cancel it performs, stamped `extra_data.reason = "material_returned"` — deliberately **not** the work-order-delete cancel reason, because `reopen_allocations_cancelled_by_delete` resurrects exactly the ties whose most recent DELETE row carries *that* reason, and a mis-stamp would bring back a tie somebody explicitly gave the material back on.
- **A suppressed backflush is recorded, not silent** (PR 4). When `_drop_ledger_covered_parts` declines to issue a BOM line because the ledger already shows that part consumed against the work order's operations, it writes a tamper-evident **`BACKFLUSH_DOUBLE_ISSUE_BLOCKED`** row carrying the suppressed quantity and the ledger net. The system declining to move material a planner's BOM asked for is precisely the shape of control gap an AS9100D 8.5.2 as-built review cannot reconstruct after the fact, and "correct but invisible" is not good enough for it. The row is bounded, not per-request: it fires on the completion paths and on a reconcile pass that actually applies a work-order transition — the same cardinality as `BACKFLUSH_SHORTAGE` — never on an ordinary read. **It has not fired in production**, because no part has opted into the leg that emits it. That used to be guaranteed by the flag having no writer; **since PR 4.5 it is a statement about data, not about code**, and it stops being true the first time a part opts in and a work order for it completes. What did **not** change is the cardinality rule: PR 4.5's dry-run preview resolves the same suppression and writes **no** row for it, because the recording function is separate from the resolution function and only the completion path calls it.
  - **PR 4.4 sharpened what "suppressed" means, and the recorded quantity changed with it.** A suppression row now always carries the demand that **did not move** — the unmet remainder `delta`, plus the `ledger_net` that authorized the decision — never a gross `qty_planned` that was already satisfied. The reason is the reordering: on the work-order-scoped tie leg the delta is computed **before** the legacy fence is consulted, so a tie that has already **converged** (its ledger net covers its plan) now `continue`s silently rather than writing a row claiming its whole plan was dropped. That is not a weakening of "recorded, not silent" — it is the claim being made true. Nothing was suppressed in that case, and the row it used to write stated a false figure on a tamper-evident chain, which is worse than no row. Every path where demand genuinely goes unmet still consults the fence and still records. The BOM leg deliberately keeps the opposite order — its suppression layers *produce* the target the delta is measured against, so they cannot run after it.
- **Arming the leg is itself an audited state change** (PR 4.5). Flipping `Part.backflush_components` is a permanent, shop-wide policy change on a tenant table, so it goes through `AuditService.log_update` like any other part edit — and the row carries more than the new value: `extra_data` records the **readiness verdict that authorized it** (`clean` / `not_evaluated_disable`, the timestamp, and the advisory codes outstanding at the time). The verdict is what an assessor cannot reconstruct later, because the BOM it judged is editable afterwards by other people. **What the row does not carry is a reason** — that is an accepted residual of the owner's choice of the ordinary part-edit field over a dedicated reasoned verb, alongside the Supervisor-tier gate and the absence of a 409 on concurrent flips (`Part` maps no `version` column). See [Exposing the flag](#exposing-the-flag-pr-45).
- **The two backflush reads write nothing, structurally** (PR 4.5). `GET /parts/{id}/backflush-readiness` and `GET /work-orders/{id}/backflush-preview` produce no ledger row, no `audit_log` row and no operational event. This is the same boundary `material_tie_view.py` was created to hold — a poll is not an actor and records no reason — and it is enforced by shape rather than by care: the resolution layer takes no `AuditService` at all, and the suppression **recording** was split out into a separate function that only the completion path calls. A preview that took the recording path would both pollute the hash chain with rows describing nothing that happened and falsify a documented cardinality.
- **A failed component write is recorded, not swallowed** (PR 4.4). Both non-indexed ISSUE legs post with `duplicate_is_noop=False`, so a real `IntegrityError` raises into a per-component / per-allocation savepoint instead of being mistaken for a concurrent duplicate and reported as a shortage. The rollback writes a tamper-evident **`BACKFLUSH_COMPONENT_FAILED`** row (the backflush's twin of `ALLOCATION_CONSUMPTION_FAILED`), the other components are unaffected, and the outer transaction stays committable so a reconcile-on-read GET still returns 200. Silently under-consuming while recording a shortage would have put a false cause on the record.
- **A skipped held lot is disclosed on the shortage row — and the disclosure names the ACTUAL constraint** (PR 4.4). The unpinned draw no longer consumes `on_hold` / `quarantine` / `rejected` stock (AS9100D 8.7); instead `BACKFLUSH_SHORTAGE` / `ALLOCATION_SHORTAGE` carry `held_quantity_skipped` + `held_lot_numbers` in `extra_data` and in the description, so a shortage is never reported bare against material physically on the rack in segregated status. **On a PINNED draw the disclosure is different, deliberately:** the reason other lots were not drawn is the **pin**, not their status, so the row carries `pinned_lot` and reads *"draw restricted to pinned lot X, other stock not eligible"* — naming held lots there would send an MRB reviewer to release quarantined stock that the pin excludes anyway, while saying nothing about the freely-available stock the pin also excluded. The two clauses are mutually exclusive by construction (`shortage_draw_disclosure` decides which applies). `HELD_MATERIAL_CONSUMED` now has exactly one meaning: a **pinned** lot held after it was pinned.
  **What this disclosure does NOT reach is the as-built record.** Both the held pair and `pinned_lot` live in `audit_log` and the event payload only; `_reconstruct_consumed_components` reads `inventory_transactions`, so a genealogy line never mentions a lot that was skipped. Where a part's stock is wholly held, the as-built line is a **lot-less placeholder** naming no heat — where it previously named the held lot. Recorded as an owner-acceptance item, not a defect: see [Open questions](#open-questions-carried-into-pr-45) item 6.
- **`material.backflush_shortage` is now a real notification** (PR 4.4). The `backflush_shortage` event has been emitted since Batch 6 with no catalog entry, and the outbox tee ignores uncataloged event types by design — so a BOM shortage has been recorded on the hash chain but notified to nobody. The catalog entry closes that. The `BACKFLUSH_SHORTAGE` `audit_log` row, not the notification, remains the compliance record.
- Lot genealogy was extended to read the new reference type **in the same PR that introduced it**. There is deliberately no release in which consumption exists but traceability cannot see it. **PR 4.4 held to the same rule for the third shape**: `work_order_backflush` arrives through the shared `work_order_ledger_filter` in the same change that starts writing it, and `traceability.py` resolves it with no code change at all (its `reference_id` already *is* the work order). One logical draw that spilled across three lots reads as three genealogy lines naming three heats — which is the point of the change, and is truthful where the single summed row was not. PR 3 held to the same rule for the RETURN row: the as-built reconstruction nets returns and **drops a (WO, part, lot) line that nets to nothing**, because a returned lot was never built into the part. Adding the type without flipping the sign would have been *worse* than omitting it — the aggregation took `abs()`, so a credit would have read as more consumption.
- **Job cost reads the new reference type too**, for the same reason. `completion_cost_service` and `analytics_service` share one predicate (`work_order_ledger_filter`) spanning `work_order`, `work_order_backflush` (PR 4.4) *and* `work_order_operation`, so tied consumption lands in `WorkOrder.actual_cost`, the synced `JobCost`, and the analytics variance. Filtering on `work_order` alone would have dropped an entire nest's material — six $80 sheets is $480 — out of compliance-facing cost reports while the ledger and audit rows said otherwise. (PR 5's `CostEntry(MATERIAL)` work is still the structural fix; this is the correctness one.) **PR 3 extended both to NET returns**, and note what the shared predicate did and did not buy: a RETURN row arrives through it unbidden (same reference shape), so the two services could not have *missed* it — but they shared no **type** predicate, so each had to flip the sign itself or a credit would have added cost under `abs()`. Both now carry the identical CASE; keep them in step.
- Every new query is tenant-scoped; a tie referencing another company's lot, part, operation, or work order is refused with a 404 (never a 403, so an id cannot be probed).
- Allocation rows are never physically deleted on the tie lifecycle (untie and all three cancellation paths — manual untie, work-order delete, and PR 3's `return_and_untie` — set `status`), so the ledger's `allocation_id` back-reference always resolves. The one exception is a work-order **hard delete**, which is refused outright if the ledger references any tie on the work order.
- No backfill. Pre-feature work orders simply have no allocations, pre-existing ledger rows keep a NULL `allocation_id`, and their existing history stands as written. **PR 4.4 kept that rule where it cost something to keep:** existing `('work_order', ISSUE)` rows are **not** re-keyed to `work_order_backflush`. Doing so would mutate regulated, hash-chain-adjacent records that no audit row covers and would silently move history between reference shapes. The legacy fence achieves the same safety in code, forever — which is also why the change is **correct-forward and deploy-order-free**: a v-old instance writes the legacy shape (which v-new fences) and a v-new instance writes the new shape (which v-old cannot see, and cannot reach, since a completed work order is never re-entered).
- `qty_consumed` on the tie is a **cache**, and since PR 3 it is no longer monotonic-up — a return lowers it, with float dust clamped to exactly 0. The authoritative consumed total in a compliance answer is the **signed** sum of `inventory_transactions` carrying that `allocation_id`: ISSUE minus RETURN. All three guards that must not be fooled by the cache read the ledger instead — hard delete since PR 1, nest re-import since PR 3, and the manual untie since PR 4. **Since PR 4.4 the column means two different things by tie shape, and the ledger rule is what makes that safe to live with:** a **work-order-scoped** tie's `qty_consumed` is written from its own signed ledger net (`_advance_tie_consumed`), so cache == net; an **operation-scoped** tie's is still the engine's run-scaled `target`, which converges on reconciliation rather than being re-read. Neither is the answer to a compliance question — the ledger is.
- **Cost of an untied work order.** Two numbers, and PR 2.5 changed one of them — state both so the next reader does not score it as a regression. (a) **`apply_completion_inventory_effects` still costs exactly one tenant-scoped SELECT** against `work_order_material_allocations`, issued once at work-order completion and threaded into every leg. That is unchanged (it was three separate reads of the same table: two unconditional — the consume engine and the work-order-scoped demand — plus a third when the part opted into backflush). (b) **An untied work order now additionally pays one tenant-scoped SELECT per operation completion**, from the new `apply_operation_completion_inventory_effects` seam, which returns `[]` and short-circuits. **Writes are still zero**: no ledger row, no audit row, no event. Invariant 6(d) — an untied work order stays byte-identical to its pre-feature behavior — is a statement about *writes* and is intact and still test-asserted; the added read is a per-operation read, not a per-run one, and it replaces nothing that was already there.
- **One ledger predicate, one place.** `work_order_ledger_filter` (and the **three** `reference_type` constants — `work_order`, `work_order_backflush`, `work_order_operation`) live in `app/db/ledger_filter.py`, not in the consumption engine. Adding the third shape in PR 4.4 cost **one line** there and no change in any reader, which is the payoff that homing it centrally was for. It answers a generic question — "which ledger rows belong to this work order?" — that job costing, analytics, lot genealogy and `GET /inventory/transactions?work_order_id=` all ask; homed in the engine it forced each of them to import the whole engine (transitively `completion_inventory_service` and `operational_event_service`) just to get a WHERE clause, and traceability had already re-implemented it with string literals rather than pay that. All four now share it, so an as-built record and the cost of the job it describes cannot disagree about what the job consumed.
- **The deprecated `POST /inventory/issue` can no longer write work-order-attributed rows** (2026-07-31, from the inventory audit). Its `work_order_number` field produced `reference_type='work_order'` + `reference_number` with a **NULL `reference_id`** — a shape `work_order_ledger_filter` cannot resolve, so the movement was invisible to job costing, lot genealogy, analytics, and the backflush suppression nets while looking attributed in the raw ledger. Sending `work_order_number` is now refused **400**; the endpoint's rows post with no reference at all, and the supported paths are a material tie (consumption posts through the completion flows) or `POST /inventory/adjust`. Existing id-less rows stand as written (no backfill) and stay matched by the local `reference_number` clause on `GET /inventory/transactions?work_order_id=`.
